## 0. Constitution, purpose and scope

### 0.1 Governing question

Phase 0 assigned Phase 4 the question: **What causal architecture supports continuity and cognition?** Phase 4 defines the architectural conditions under which the NNNC may represent, generate, trace, test and revise causal relations among events, components, states, processes and boundary interactions. It does not assert that any proposed NNNC implementation already realizes cognition, intelligence or verified causal understanding.

### 0.2 Inheritance and precedence

P4 SHALL preserve the NNNC–NES separation, Phase 0 heartbeat/recovery and protected-operation boundaries, Phase 1 entity/constituent/qualification and five-flow/six-algorithm commitments, Phase 2 identity/continuity and causal-history distinctions, and Phase 3 constitutional authorization, entrenchment and competence boundaries. Phase 3 D1 v0.2.6 is read with adopted C1. Phase 3 D2 v0.5.2 is a *conditionally adopted* mathematical baseline; its residual obligations remain open. Where this candidate conflicts with a governing predecessor, the predecessor controls until a separately authorized amendment.

**Predecessor-status clarification.**

The adopted Phase 3 C1 addendum and the conditional adoption of NNNC-P3-D2 v0.5.2 are distinct governance dispositions. Recognition of C1 as adopted SHALL NOT be interpreted as discharge of the mathematical package's residual obligations, including PAC-2.

Phase 4 inherits the applicable authorization relations and their established scopes. It SHALL NOT redefine general protected authorization as lifecycle-specific authorization, or conversely, without a separately authorized predecessor amendment.

### 0.3 Normative vocabulary and epistemic discipline

**SHALL** and **SHALL NOT** are mandatory; **SHOULD** is defeasible only by recorded justification; **MAY** is permitted. **DEF** defines a term, **REQ** imposes an obligation, **ASM** declares an assumption, **HYP** marks a hypothesis, **EPI** restricts a warranted claim, and **DEFER** identifies later work. A proposed causal graph, simulation, statistical association or fitted model is not by itself proof of actual causation. Claims SHALL identify their scope, evidence and uncertainty.

### 0.4 Non-goals

This document does not prescribe a particular neural architecture, causal discovery algorithm, probabilistic programming language, training procedure, embodiment, hardware clock, real-time scheduler, cognitive phenomenology, consciousness criterion or physical-effect safety certification. Phase 5 specifies FFNN realization; Phase 6 specifies Systemic Algorithm realization; Phase 7 specifies NES runtime guarantees; later phases govern intrinsic motivation, agency and self-modification. This phase SHALL provide compatible interfaces rather than substitute its own implementations.

## 1. Causal ontology and distinct relations

**DEF-P4-001 — Causal occurrence.** An occurrence is a realized event or transition in a system or environment. Its occurrence does not depend on observation, recording, successful identification of its locus, or knowledge of its temporal or causal placement.

A representation or record may refer to an actual, alleged, hypothetical or simulated occurrence. These referential statuses SHALL remain distinguishable.

Claims concerning an occurrence SHALL identify the applicable realization and history context and SHALL declare available information about locus, placement and observation status, including unknown or disputed information. Missing information SHALL NOT by itself establish non-occurrence. A description or record SHALL NOT by itself establish occurrence.

**DEF-P4-002 — Causal influence.** A source variable, condition, mechanism or occurrence has causal influence on a specified target when, under declared causal semantics and an admissible comparison, variation in the source can change the target's occurrence, distribution, trajectory or other specified property. Influence SHALL be qualified by the applicable model, background conditions, comparison class and target property. Influence under one admissible context SHALL NOT establish influence in every context. A model-relative influence claim does not by itself establish that the source contributed to a particular realized outcome.

**DEF-P4-003 — Causal dependency.** A dependency is a specified condition required for an event, transition, computation or explanation within a declared scope. Necessity in one mechanism or model SHALL NOT be generalized to universal necessity.

**DEF-P4-004 — Causal provenance.** Provenance is the evidence-backed history of relevant generating, transforming, transmitting and recording operations. Provenance, authority, custody and individual identity remain distinct relations.

**DEF-P4-005 — Causal structure.** A causal structure is a typed representation of variables/occurrences, dependency or influence relations, applicable contexts, interventions, uncertainty, and versioned model assumptions. It may be hierarchical, cyclic in feedback descriptions, or partially ordered at the occurrence level; these are not interchangeable structures.

**DEF-P4-006 — Causal claim status.** A causal claim is classified as *proposed*, *supported under stated assumptions*, *refuted under stated assumptions*, or *undetermined*. These are evidence-relative assessments, not omniscient historical truth values.

**DEF-P4-007 — Realized causal domain.** The actual system/environment occurrences, operations, transitions and effects under a specified realization and history. Its existence and behavior are not established by an NNNC's description of them. An architectural specification constrains admissible realizations but does not itself demonstrate an actual implementation.

**DEF-P4-008 — Representational causal domain.** A model-bearing system's encoded hypotheses, causal graphs, predictions, explanations, simulations and counterfactual descriptions. Representation may be absent, mistaken or incomplete even when an actual process occurs.

**DEF-P4-009 — Evidential causal domain.** Observations, instruments, records, tests, interventions, provenance and adjudications used to assess causal claims. Evidential status is not identical to realized historical truth; a forged or faulty record cannot create its alleged occurrence.

**DEF-P4-010 — Causal abstraction.** A declared mapping from lower-level occurrences or mechanisms to a higher-level causal description, specifying the scope and preserved causal properties. A useful abstraction need not list every low-level event, but its causal adequacy must be independently assessable.

**DEF-P4-011 — Intervention.** An explicitly scoped alteration or hypothetical replacement of a variable, condition, mechanism or boundary input under stated assumptions. Epistemic, computational, environmental and constitutionally protected classifications may overlap; none is a permission grant.

**DEF-P4-012 — Causal plasticity.** Change over time in either (a) a representational causal model or (b) a realized mechanism. The two kinds of change are separate and may occur independently.

**DEF-P4-013 — Causal role.** A specified model-relative or mechanism-relative role such as necessary, sufficient, contributing, enabling, inhibiting or competing condition. Role assertions require a defined outcome, background context and intervention or comparison semantics; they are not unrestricted universal statements.

**DEF-P4-014 — Positive causal organization.** The organization of realized internal mechanisms, state and dependencies through which an NNNC's architecturally required or additionally claimed capabilities are produced, coordinated and sustained across declared operating conditions. It requires specified causally consequential relationships between internal processes. Component names, communication links, shared storage, common administration and descriptive models do not by themselves establish these relationships. Organization MAY be distributed, asynchronous, redundant, deterministic, stochastic or hybrid; this definition requires neither a central controller nor continuous execution, a single global processing cycle, or a particular neural topology. The existence of a realization contract is distinct from actual realization and evidence-based assessment.

**REQ-P4-001 — Non-conflation.** P4 SHALL distinguish temporal succession, correlation, causal influence, logical implication, computational data dependency, authorization, custody, organizational integration and identity. None alone substitutes for another.

**REQ-P4-002 — Typed referents.** Each causal relation SHALL identify the kinds of its endpoints (e.g. event, state property, component operation, governance occurrence or boundary interaction), its context, and its evidential status. Phase 1 and Phase 2 native sorts SHALL NOT be silently merged.

**REQ-P4-003 — Event/state distinction.** Persistent states, transitions between states, observations of those states, and records of transitions SHALL remain distinguishable. A change in a representation does not automatically establish a corresponding change in its represented environment.

**DEF-P4-015 — Actual causal contribution.** The role attributed to an instantiated source condition, occurrence, mechanism or mechanism group in producing, enabling, inhibiting or otherwise shaping a particular realized outcome, under declared causal-attribution semantics and background conditions. An actual-contribution claim SHALL identify the realized target, relevant source instances, applicable mechanism/model correspondence and treatment of redundancy, joint sufficiency, compensation and preemption where relevant. Failure of a single-source removal test SHALL NOT alone establish absence of contribution. Membership in a sufficient set SHALL NOT alone establish individual contribution without the further conditions required by the declared attribution semantics. The attribution rule SHALL be explicit and need not be universal.

## 2. Causal order, time and feedback

**REQ-P4-004 — Partial order.** For historical occurrence claims, established causal precedence SHALL be distinguished from concurrency, merely observed ordering and unknown order. A missing proven precedence edge SHALL NOT establish concurrency. Applicable Phase 2 causal-history rules remain controlling.

**REQ-P4-005 — Feedback without self-origin.**

Feedback mechanisms MAY be represented as recurrent or cyclic at the component, variable, or dynamical abstraction level.

Claims asserting a concrete realized occurrence-level causal history SHALL identify the applicable occurrence domain, governing execution semantics, and strict causal-predecessor relation used to interpret that history.

The asserted concrete occurrence-level causal-predecessor relation SHALL be well-founded: every nonempty subset of its declared occurrence domain SHALL contain an occurrence having no strict causal predecessor within that subset. Equivalently, under the standard dependent-choice assumptions for the relevant relation, the relation SHALL admit no infinite strictly descending chain of causal predecessors.

Such a relation SHALL exclude self-originating occurrences and causal-dependency cycles among individual occurrences. Acyclicity alone SHALL NOT be treated as sufficient to establish well-foundedness.

The causal-dependency relation governing the asserted realized history SHALL remain distinct from evidence-supported assessments of causal precedence under REQ-P4-004. An unknown causal order SHALL NOT be treated as established concurrency.

Continuous-time, recurrent, or other idealized dynamical models SHALL NOT be prohibited solely because their mathematical representation is cyclic, continuous, or lacks a discrete first time point.

Where an idealized dynamical model is used to make claims about concrete realized occurrences, the relevant occurrence-level interpretation and correspondence SHALL be declared. An abstract feedback cycle SHALL NOT establish that any individual occurrence caused itself.

P4-D2 SHALL formalize the applicable occurrence relation, its well-foundedness conditions, and the distinction between abstract dynamical representation and concrete occurrence history.

**REQ-P4-006 — Delay and latency.** Causal descriptions SHALL allow delayed effects, queued events, asynchronous observations, and absent acknowledgments without assuming global synchronous time or instantaneous propagation.

**REQ-P4-007 — Competing effects.** When multiple candidate causes or mutually exclusive outcomes compete, the architecture SHALL represent the competing paths and evidence; it SHALL NOT silently select one as historically actual merely because it is locally feasible.

**REQ-P4-008 — No retroactive authorization.** Later observations, outcomes or model updates SHALL NOT retroactively supply the strict-prior constitutional authorization required for a protected occurrence.

## 3. Mechanisms, interventions and uncertainty

**REQ-P4-009 — Mechanism specification.** A claimed causal mechanism SHALL specify its input conditions, affected variables or operations, mediating dependencies, boundary conditions and possible failure modes at a level appropriate to its claim.

**REQ-P4-010 — Observation versus intervention.** The architecture SHALL distinguish passive observation, deliberate intervention, naturally occurring perturbation and simulated intervention. Observational associations alone SHALL NOT be labeled experimentally verified effects.

**REQ-P4-011 — Counterfactual discipline.** Counterfactual claims SHALL name the model, intervention, held-fixed assumptions, comparison outcome and scope of validity. Unidentifiable counterfactuals SHALL remain undetermined rather than receive fabricated values.

**REQ-P4-012 — Uncertainty.** Causal assessments SHALL represent uncertainty arising from incomplete observations, hidden common causes, model misspecification, nondeterminism, competing explanations, and measurement error where relevant. A numeric confidence score SHALL NOT substitute for an articulated evidence contract.

**REQ-P4-013 — Falsifiability and revision.** Claims SHALL admit identifiable challenges or disconfirming evidence where their semantics permit. Model revisions SHALL preserve historical provenance and distinguish changed interpretation from changed history.

## 4. Organization, cognition and boundary interactions

**REQ-P4-014 — Multi-level organization.** P4 SHALL accommodate causal relations at constituent, module, flow, system and external-interaction levels, with explicit mappings where an explanation crosses levels. Higher-level labels SHALL NOT erase lower-level dependencies needed for verification.

**REQ-P4-015 — Cognitive relevance without premature qualification.** The architecture SHALL support causal descriptions of perception, memory change, internal processing, learning, decision formation and action selection as candidate functions. Merely labeling a process with these names SHALL NOT establish Phase 1 qualification, FFNN realization, six-algorithm realization or intelligence.

**REQ-P4-016 — NNNC–NES boundary.** Causal accounts SHALL distinguish NNNC-owned processes from NES-provided execution, transport, persistence and resource effects. Dependence on substrate execution SHALL NOT collapse entity identity into a specific substrate or imply substrate authority over protected NNNC state.

**REQ-P4-017 — Boundary evidence.** External observations, commands, effects and feedback SHALL carry source, timing/order, provenance, reliability and trust-boundary qualifications appropriate to the causal claim. A reported physical effect is not an independently verified physical effect.

**REQ-P4-018 — Continuity compatibility.** Causal accounts of migration, replacement, branching, suspension, restoration and termination SHALL preserve Phase 2 distinctions among causal ancestry, custody, constitutive continuation and numerical identity. Causal linkage alone SHALL NOT establish identity preservation.

**REQ-P4-019 — Constitution compatibility.** Any intervention, learning operation, model revision or execution step that is a protected act SHALL satisfy applicable prior Phase 0–3 authorization; P4 causal competence or prediction accuracy SHALL NOT grant authority. Adopted C1's general protected authorization and unchanged Phase 2 lifecycle specialization apply according to effect scope.

## 5. Causal models, learning and evidence

**REQ-P4-020 — Model plurality.** The architecture MAY host deterministic, stochastic, structural, dynamical, rule-based or hybrid causal representations. It SHALL define correspondence between representations when transferring a claim and SHALL NOT presume equivalent causal meaning from equivalent predictive accuracy.

**REQ-P4-021 — Model provenance.** Each operative causal model SHALL have a traceable version, applicable context, provenance of supporting evidence, known limitations and compatibility relation to predecessor constraints. A self-generated assertion SHALL NOT count as independent validation.

**REQ-P4-022 — Safe learning boundary.** Learning or updating causal models MAY alter epistemic state within authorized scope. Changing protected governance, lifecycle state, authority, constitutional rules or real external resources requires its own applicable authorization; an inferred causal advantage cannot bypass that boundary.

**REQ-P4-023 — Error and recovery semantics.** The architecture SHALL represent mistaken attribution, missing effects, false observations, stale evidence, contradictory measurements, causal-model failure and recovery as distinct possible conditions, without claiming that any particular recovery implementation is already safe.

**REQ-P4-024 — Independent challenge.** A causal claim proposed by a component SHALL be challengeable through evidence or tests not defined solely by that component's own assertion. Independence claims SHALL be evaluated under Phase 3's material-control and provenance constraints.

## 5A. Round A normative refinements — domains, abstraction, interventions and plasticity

**REQ-P4-025 — Domain separation.** Every causal claim SHALL distinguish the realized referent, its representation (if any), and the evidence used to assess it. Evidence or model state SHALL NOT be silently promoted into an actual effect. Where a referent is only hypothetical or not yet implemented, the claim SHALL say so.

**REQ-P4-026 — Domain correspondence.** Claims that a representation tracks a realized mechanism SHALL identify a correspondence relation, observation limits and applicable context. Correct prediction alone SHALL NOT establish correct mechanism identification; lack of an internal explanation alone SHALL NOT negate an actual mechanism.

**REQ-P4-027 — Abstraction adequacy.**

A cross-level causal claim SHALL declare its abstraction map or maps, the admissible contexts, interventions, and observables for which adequacy is asserted, the causal outcomes or dependencies required to be preserved, and the conditions under which adequacy fails or remains undetermined.

Adequacy SHALL be assessed relative to the declared claim and comparison domain. Preservation established for one intervention class, context, or observable set SHALL NOT automatically establish preservation outside that domain.

Claims of predictive adequacy, intervention adequacy, causal-mechanism identification, and explanatory adequacy SHALL remain distinguishable according to their declared purposes and supporting conditions.

Predictive adequacy SHALL NOT independently establish intervention adequacy, causal-mechanism identification, or explanatory adequacy.

Different abstraction mappings MAY be employed for different declared purposes, provided their domains, correspondence assumptions, and claimed preserved properties are identified. No common mapping or cross-purpose equivalence SHALL be presumed without justification.

A higher-level name, including memory, self, agency, or intelligence, SHALL NOT constitute a free-standing causal explanation or independent authority source.

**REQ-P4-028 — Emergent organization.** System-level causal properties MAY be modeled when supported by declared organization and abstraction semantics. Emergence SHALL NOT be treated as proof of an extra entity, intrinsic agency, consciousness, numerical identity or a mechanism independent of the realization.

**REQ-P4-029 — Intervention typing and effect-derived scope.** An intervention SHALL identify its target, context, intended changes, relevant possible side effects, epistemic or realized status and applicable predecessor authorization requirements. Applicable protected scopes SHALL be derived from operations and resources the intervention can affect, including indirect effects through invoked components, services and substrate operations. Caller-supplied labels SHALL NOT be the sole basis of scope determination. Before execution, effect analysis SHALL establish conservative coverage of relevant possible effects under declared assumptions; unresolved effects SHALL NOT be treated as absent. Realized-effect records, where available, SHALL remain distinct from intended effects and pre-execution analysis and SHALL NOT retroactively supply missing prior authorization.

**REQ-P4-030 — Non-equivalence of intervention classes.** Describing, simulating or evaluating an intervention SHALL NOT establish permission or capacity to execute its represented effects. The computation performing simulation or evaluation may itself have realized effects; those effects SHALL be assessed independently of the hypothetical represented effects. Any protected effect of the computation or intervention SHALL satisfy applicable strict-prior Phase 0–3 authorization requirements. Lifecycle-affecting operations SHALL retain unchanged Phase 2 authorization arguments and adopted C1 specialization under governing predecessor contracts. If effect coverage or required authorization remains unresolved, the intervention SHALL NOT receive a positive protected-execution authorization assessment. A realized effect outside assessed coverage SHALL be recorded as a coverage failure and evaluated under inherited violation and recovery rules. Finite-model coverage, assumption-supported engineering assurance and deployed implementation assurance SHALL NOT be conflated.

**REQ-P4-031 — Learning versus mechanism change.** A model update SHALL be distinguished from alteration of realized routing, parameters, components or other mechanisms. Claims of adaptive causal improvement SHALL specify which changed and what evidence supports the claim.

**REQ-P4-032 — Plasticity governance.** Actual mechanism adaptation SHALL preserve inherited identity, continuity, qualification and constitutional constraints. Model revision SHALL NOT rewrite historical occurrences; mechanism adaptation SHALL NOT inherit permission solely from epistemic update authority.

**Normative clarification to REQ-P4-031 and REQ-P4-032 — Overlapping plasticity.**

A single operation can simultaneously revise a representational causal model and alter a realized computational or physical mechanism. Recognition of this possible overlap does not constitute permission to perform the operation.

Where both effects occur, the operation SHALL retain both classifications, and every independently applicable constraint SHALL be evaluated according to the affected state, operation, and governing scope.

Authorization for epistemic model revision SHALL NOT substitute for authorization independently required for protected mechanism changes.

Where applicable constraints cannot be jointly satisfied, the operation SHALL NOT proceed as an authorized protected operation. A less restrictive classification SHALL NOT override a stricter applicable predecessor constraint.

An ordinary model update SHALL NOT be classified as a protected constitutional or lifecycle transition solely because it changes computational behavior. Its protected status SHALL be determined by applicable predecessor definitions, affected state, and actual or reasonably anticipated effects.

This clarification SHALL preserve the distinction between representational revision, realized mechanism change, historical occurrence, and constitutional authorization.

P4-D2 SHALL include a verification case involving overlapping classifications and jointly applicable constraints, including a case in which authorization conditions cannot all be satisfied.

**REQ-P4-033 — Causal-role qualification.** Necessary, sufficient, contributing, enabling, inhibiting and competing causes SHALL be evaluated relative to an explicit outcome and background conditions. An identified contributor SHALL NOT be asserted sufficient without supporting semantics; an unobserved cause SHALL NOT be asserted absent. A causal-role claim SHALL distinguish potential influence from contribution to a specified realized outcome. Where redundancy, joint sufficiency, compensation, preemption or context dependence is relevant, the claim SHALL declare how its attribution semantics handles that condition. Neither single-source removal nor membership in a successful group SHALL be a universal criterion for individual contribution. If attribution semantics are unspecified or evidence fails to discriminate relevant alternatives, the contribution assessment SHALL remain undetermined.

**REQ-P4-034 — Identifiability and underdetermination.** Where different causal structures remain compatible with the evidence, the architecture SHALL preserve the alternatives or return an undetermined assessment. Model-fit or confidence SHALL NOT collapse observational equivalence into unique causal truth.

## 5B. Positive causal organization and capability realization — ED-P4-008

This section is an architect-directed substantive expansion of the proposed Round C candidate. It adds positive obligations without superseding the existing causal-claim, authorization, identity or qualification rules.

**REQ-P4-035 — Positive causal organization contract.**

An NNNC architectural realization SHALL specify the internal causal organization supporting every applicable internally realized capability. Each capability realization contract SHALL identify (a) its operational meaning, modes and operating conditions; (b) participating internal mechanisms and state; (c) dependencies among mechanisms, state and outcomes; (d) causal contributions of relied-upon mechanisms or mechanism groups; (e) external contributions and NES dependencies; (f) conditions of maintenance, degradation, unavailability and uncertainty; and (g) discriminating comparisons and evidence for assessment.

For every capability claimed as internally realized, an internal mechanism or mechanism group SHALL make a causally consequential contribution to its decisive processing. Invocation, transport, logging, or output formatting alone SHALL NOT establish internal realization.

Applicability is determined independently of realization-contract validity and outcome under §5B.1. Applicable coverage SHALL include both the roles and capabilities required by governing NNNC architectural and qualification contracts and any additional capabilities asserted by the candidate. Omitting a claim SHALL NOT waive a mandatory architectural obligation. An explicitly partial research prototype MAY be evaluated against a declared partial scope but SHALL NOT be represented as a fully qualified NNNC on that basis.

**REQ-P4-036 — State-mediated processing and memory.**

The causal organization SHALL include internal state whose value or organization can affect subsequent internal processing within a declared operating context. Where retained state is claimed or architecturally required to perform a memory function, the realization contract SHALL identify how it influences subsequent processing and under what conditions. Recording, retaining or retrieving information without a specified consequential role in subsequent processing SHALL NOT alone establish memory realization. Participating state SHALL remain distinct from observations and records describing processing. 'Subsequent' SHALL respect the applicable occurrence relation and SHALL NOT presume a universal clock.

**REQ-P4-037 — Coordinated internal processing.**

The causal organization SHALL specify dependencies through which relevant internal mechanisms participate in coordinated processing. For each capability requiring integration, the realization contract SHALL identify how contributions combine, constrain or alter relevant processing or outcomes. Communication, shared storage or a common interface SHALL NOT alone establish causal integration. Integration SHALL be assessed at the scope of the claimed capability; evidence for isolated mechanisms or pairs SHALL NOT automatically establish whole-capability organization. Graph connectivity alone SHALL NOT establish coordination.

**REQ-P4-038 — Regulation and feedback.**

The causal organization SHALL include internal regulatory relationships capable of influencing selection, continuation, inhibition or allocation of internal processing. The realization contract SHALL identify regulatory conditions, targets and consequences, and specify combination or resolution where competing regulatory conditions arise.

The organization SHALL include feedback relationships through which results of processing or interaction can influence subsequent internal processing. Claims of realized feedback SHALL identify distinct occurrences and a causal-dependency interpretation satisfying REQ-P4-005; an abstract cycle SHALL NOT establish occurrence self-causation.

These requirements establish neither intrinsic motivation nor autonomous agency nor authority to perform protected effects.

**REQ-P4-039 — Causal continuity obligations.**

For each applicable capability subject to interruption, restoration, migration, replacement or authorized modification, its realization contract SHALL identify the state and causal relationships required for continuation. The contract SHALL specify relationships that must be preserved, may be reconstructed, may change within declared limits or may be temporarily unavailable. Restoring stored data alone SHALL NOT establish restoration of the capability; applicable causal relationships and conditions must also be established. Capability continuity SHALL remain distinct from entity identity, custody and authorization; Phase 2 governs identity and continuity determinations.

**REQ-P4-040 — Discriminating realization evidence.**

Each positive causal organization claim SHALL specify comparisons capable, under declared assumptions, of discriminating its causal contribution from relevant alternatives. Comparisons MAY concern mechanisms, mechanism groups, trajectories, intermediate outcomes, timing, robustness or final outcomes. A single-component ablation SHALL NOT be universally required: redundancy, compensation, stochasticity and context dependence may make other comparisons necessary.

Each comparison SHALL declare its assumptions, context, permissible interventions, measured distinctions and evidential limits. A comparison design SHALL NOT grant authority to execute an intervention. Inaccessible or prohibited interventions SHALL NOT be presumed performed. Inconclusive evidence SHALL yield an undetermined realization assessment rather than establish either realization or absence.

### 5B.1 Bounded realization contract and formal handoff

The following is a **D1 interface sketch**, not a completed D2 formal calculus. Let \(S,K,C,M,V,O,R,E\) denote candidate systems, capabilities, operating contexts (including mode/version), mechanisms, state variables, occurrences, realization contracts and evidence records, respectively. A contract \(r\in R\) declares finite, typed descriptions of relevant mechanisms, state, dependencies and discriminating comparisons, without assuming that its declared mechanisms actually exist.

\[
\operatorname{ContractWellFormed}(r,k,c)
\]

requires the seven fields in REQ-P4-035 and the governing architectural coverage. Distinguish the semantic predicate \(\operatorname{SatisfiesOrganization}(s,r,c)\) from the evidence-relative assessment:

\[
\operatorname{OrganizationAssessment}(s,r,c,F)
\in\{\mathrm{SUPPORTED},\mathrm{REFUTED},\mathrm{UNDETERMINED}\},\quad F\subseteq E.
\]

These three assessment values are local to realization assessment and SHALL be mapped explicitly to the four general causal-claim statuses of DEF-P4-006 by P4-D2; they SHALL NOT silently replace those statuses. A well-formed contract, model prediction, or evidence record SHALL NOT by itself establish satisfaction.

Contribution SHALL distinguish \(\operatorname{ModelContribution}_{\mu}(s,A,Y,c)\) under declared model \(\mu\), \(\operatorname{RealizedContribution}_{\alpha,\mu}(s,A,y,w,c)\) concerning actual mechanisms under declared attribution semantics \(\alpha\), and evidence-relative assessment of the latter. Potential influence \(\operatorname{Influences}_{\mu}(A,Y,c)\) is not the same predicate as actual contribution \(\operatorname{ActualContribution}_{\alpha,\mu}(A,y,w,c)\). Here \(A\) is a state-bearing part or mechanism group and \(Y\) is a specified processing property or outcome. Neither contribution relation SHALL be defined circularly by \(\operatorname{MinimumOrganization}\) or \(\operatorname{SatisfiesOrganization}\).

An admissible **model-relative witness**, not a universal ablation definition, may be:

\[
\exists i_0,i_1\in\operatorname{AdmissibleComparisons}_{\mu}(A,c):
\operatorname{Outcome}_{\mu}(Y\mid i_0,c)
\not\approx_{r,Y,c}
\operatorname{Outcome}_{\mu}(Y\mid i_1,c).
\]

The contract SHALL specify the changed and controlled conditions, outcome type (value, trajectory or distribution), and relevance relation \(\not\approx_{r,Y,c}\). Realized contribution requires a separately justified correspondence between model, comparison and realized mechanisms; no model-relative witness automatically establishes it.

P4-D2 SHALL give independently interpretable semantics to \(\operatorname{StateMediated}\), \(\operatorname{MemoryRealized}\), \(\operatorname{RegulationRealized}\), \(\operatorname{FeedbackRealized}\), and \(\operatorname{CoordinationRealized}\), preserving the following D1 constraints: state must affect subsequent processing; retained memory state must have a consequential later role; regulatory conditions must affect processing dispositions; feedback must have distinct well-founded occurrence witnesses; and coordination must concern capability-relevant combining or constraining relationships rather than graph connectivity alone.

\operatorname{MinimumOrganization}(s,c)\iff {}&
\operatorname{StateMediated}(s,c)\\
&\land\operatorname{RegulationRealized}(s,c)\\

## 6. Architectural invariants and prohibited shortcuts

**INV-P4-01 — No identity from causation.** Causal ancestry or similarity alone never entails numerical identity.

**INV-P4-02 — No authority from causation.** Causal effectiveness, competence or predicted utility never entails authorization.

**INV-P4-03 — No cause from sequence.** Temporal precedence or correlation alone never entails causal influence.

**INV-P4-04 — No effect from representation.** A modeled or logged effect alone never entails actual execution or physical effect.

**INV-P4-05 — No fabricated order.** Unknown causal ordering never entails proven precedence or proven concurrency.

**INV-P4-06 — No self-certification.** An internal model's own assertions alone never establish independent causal validation.

**INV-P4-07 — Predecessor preservation.** P4 cannot weaken inherited lifecycle, custody, constitutional, protected-operation, qualification or boundary constraints.

**INV-P4-08 — Domain non-substitution.** A causal representation or assessment cannot independently establish a realized event.

**INV-P4-09 — Abstraction accountability.** A higher-level causal attribution cannot evade declared lower-level dependencies, validation or governing constraints.

**INV-P4-10 — No simulated authority.** Epistemic or simulated intervention cannot authorize corresponding realized effects.

**INV-P4-11 — Plasticity non-equivalence.** Updating a model cannot by itself establish or authorize a change in the mechanism it models.

**INV-P4-12 — No unqualified causal-role inference.**

Establishing that a condition contributes to an outcome SHALL NOT, by itself, establish that the condition is necessary or sufficient for that outcome.

Claims of necessity, sufficiency, or contribution SHALL each identify their applicable outcome, context, and supporting causal interpretation.

An increase in confidence that a condition contributes to an outcome SHALL NOT independently justify reclassifying that condition as necessary or sufficient.
