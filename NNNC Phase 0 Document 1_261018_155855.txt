PART I — DOCUMENT CONSTITUTION
0. Purpose and Authority
0.1 Purpose
This specification establishes the architectural boundary of the Neutral Neural Network Core (NNNC).
It defines:
• What NNNC is intended to be.
• What belongs to NNNC and what remains external.
• How logical ownership differs from execution, custody, and authority.
• What constitutes declared membership and operational integration.
• How the existential heartbeat and recovery mechanisms relate to NNNC.
• How continuous operation, degradation, suspension, and termination are represented.
• How protected foundational components are governed.
• Which architectural properties must be formalized and tested.
It does not claim that the proposed architecture has already demonstrated intelligence, consciousness, life, or metaphysical individuality.
0.2 Architectural Hierarchy
The NNNC program proceeds through thirteen phases, numbered 0–12.
Phase 0 is foundational. Its requirements constrain later phases unless explicitly superseded through governed architectural revision.
Each phase produces two principal documents:
Document 1: Comprehensive architectural specification.
Document 2: Mathematical, technical, and verification formalization.
Implementation follows a sufficiently defined and reviewed contract.
0.3 Design Discipline
The project follows four principles:
Architecture first. Mechanisms second. Implementation third. Verification throughout.
Additionally:
• Confirmed design intentions must be distinguished from proposed mechanisms.
• Architectural definitions must not be confused with empirical discoveries.
• Unresolved questions must be assigned to responsible phases.
• Formalization must identify implementable obligations.
• Implementation results must be reported separately from intended behavior.
• Failed experiments must be permitted to invalidate architectural hypotheses.

PART II — FOUNDATIONAL ONTOLOGY
1. Definition of NNNC
1.1 Foundational Proposition
DEF-001 — Neutral Neural Network Core
NNNC is a proposed persistent, internally organized, developmentally adaptable artificial cognitive architecture with a distinguishable operational boundary, governed constituent organization, internal state, and capacity for ongoing activity independent of externally assigned tasks.
Its intended organization includes cognitive, existential, representational, regulatory, motivational, agentic, and developmental responsibilities.
The architecture is designed to support future investigation of autonomous cognition and developmental change.
1.2 What NNNC Is Not
NNNC shall not be defined solely as:
• A conventional artificial neural network.
• An FFNN.
• A language model.
• A reward-maximizing policy.
• A prompt-response service.
• A process identifier.
• A checkpoint or memory database.
• The NES runtime.
• An external task or environment.
• A consciousness claim.
No individual constituent is sufficient, merely by existing, to establish the full NNNC architecture.
1.3 Persistent Organization
NNNC is intended to maintain a continuing internal organization across ordinary changes in memory, internal state, cognitive processing, and authorized architectural development.
Persistence does not require every component to remain unchanged.
It does require the architecture to distinguish ordinary transformation, temporary unavailability, replacement, branching, and termination.
1.4 Continuous Operation
OB-23 — Continuous Operation by Design
NNNC shall be designed for ongoing operation as its normal condition, without depending on an external prompt, task, or request to initiate its existence.
Operational continuity means recurring progress or responsiveness under declared scheduling and resource assumptions—not uninterrupted processor execution.
Authorized suspension and shutdown remain permissible.
1.5 Neutrality
Neutrality does not mean NNNC must lack internal preferences, motivations, priorities, or evaluative mechanisms.
It means that its fundamental architecture shall not be defined exclusively by a predetermined external task, fixed reward objective, or single environment.
OB-04 — Task-Independent Architectural Boundary
The definition and membership of NNNC shall not depend on successful performance of any particular external task.
Task-independent architecture does not establish task-general intelligence.

2.3 Boundary Requirements
OB-01: NNNC shall be logically distinguishable from NES.
OB-02: NNNC shall be distinguishable from external environments and tasks.
OB-03: NNNC shall not be defined solely by FFNN.
OB-09: Reliance on NES or physical resources shall not automatically transfer logical ownership of NNNC constituents.
OB-13: Distributed execution shall not automatically establish multiple entities; shared infrastructure shall not automatically establish a single entity.
2.4 NES Is Not NNNC
NES provides computational conditions.
It may schedule NNNC-owned mechanisms, allocate resources, custody protected state, and enforce infrastructure-level policies.
Those services do not make NES the logical owner of NNNC's internal mechanisms.
Likewise, NNNC's ownership of a mechanism does not mean NNNC can execute it without substrate support.
2.5 External Environments
External environments may include games, simulations, software systems, robots, or other agents.
NNNC may receive observations and consequences from them and issue authorized actions.
External information, even if essential to NNNC's behavior, does not automatically become an NNNC constituent.
3. Membership and Integration
3.1 Declared Membership
A component belongs to NNNC at the declared architectural level when it is included in an authorized constituent manifest under the applicable membership contract.
Membership is governed by explicit registration, role, and authority rules.
OB-05 — Governed Membership
NNNC constituent membership shall be established and changed through authorized architectural transitions.
An external payload shall not establish its own membership.
3.2 Operational Integration
Declared membership does not prove that a component is functioning or integrated.
Operational integration requires evidence that the component participates in its specified causal relationships.
3.3 Constitutive Significance
A component's significance to NNNC depends on the organizational role it fulfills.
An individual implementation may be replaceable while the role it implements remains necessary.
Constitutive significance must not be inferred solely from a component's name, label, or declared membership.
3.4 Three Conformance Levels
Declared instance: A registered instance satisfying foundational identity, manifest, and governance conditions.
Operational instance: A declared instance whose required components, state, and runtime-role contracts satisfy the operational specification.
Cognitively conformant instance: An operational instance that additionally satisfies the empirical cognitive criteria established by later phases.
These classifications shall remain distinct.
An empty but valid manifest may establish a declared instance without establishing an operational or cognitively conformant one.
3.5 Integration Evidence
Causal integration requires evidence beyond communication frequency or statistical correlation.
Controlled interventions should establish whether the specified organizational relationship exists.
Unexpected dependencies shall be recorded rather than counted as successful expected integration.

PART III — STATE, OWNERSHIP, AND AUTHORITY
4. State and Responsibility Boundaries
4.1 State Categories
NNNC-related state may include:
• Constituent registry and manifests.
• Component roles and interfaces.
• Cognitive and developmental state.
• Memory and internal representations.
• Existential heartbeat state.
• Recovery state.
• Authorization policies.
• Lifecycle records.
• Resource allocations.
• Checkpoints.
• Provenance and event journals.
• Pending operations and transactions.
These categories do not require separate physical databases.
4.2 Ownership, Custody, Execution, and Authority
Four relationships shall be represented independently:

Relationship
Question

Logical ownership
To which entity or principal does this state or mechanism belong?

Physical custody
Which service stores or maintains the bytes?

Execution
Which runtime executes the mechanism?

Authority
Who may read, change, replace, or govern it?


Semantic responsibility and governance responsibility may require further distinctions.
4.3 Canonical Ownership
Registered protected state shall have a well-defined canonical owner.
Unassigned objects may exist during permitted creation or registration procedures, but protected objects shall not remain ambiguously owned.
Shared access does not imply shared canonical ownership.
4.4 Protected Mutation
Authoritative protected state shall change only through permitted operations.
Ordinary callers must not be able to mutate protected nested objects through references returned by an interface.
OB-06 — Protected State Integrity
Protected state mutations shall require authorized transitions and shall not be bypassable through ordinary reference aliasing, payload manipulation, or direct access exposed by the declared interface.
4.5 Information Is Not Authority
External content, internal cognitive proposals, and tool outputs may contain instructions.
Such content does not become an authorized control operation merely by expressing one.
OB-07 — Authority Separation
Information-bearing payloads shall not grant themselves elevated membership, modification, or governance authority.
5. Governance and Policy Integrity
5.1 Trusted Control Plane
A trusted control-plane mechanism shall govern foundational operations including:
• Instance registration.
• Membership changes.
• Protected state mutation.
• Policy modification.
• Lifecycle transitions.
• ADMIN CLASS maintenance.
• Checkpoint publication.
• Authorized restoration.
This is a logical architectural responsibility.
Its physical implementation may use NES infrastructure or other protected services.
5.2 Prior-Policy Authorization
A proposed policy change shall be authorized under the policy in force before the change commits.
A component shall not grant itself authority through the very policy update being evaluated.
5.3 Revocation
Privilege revocation shall have defined commit-order semantics.
An operation whose authority is revoked before its commit shall not be permitted to commit using the revoked privilege.
5.4 Trusted Bootstrap
The implementation shall identify how initial administrative authority is established.
Ordinary NNNC cognitive components shall not be permitted to replace the trusted authority root through their own modification operations.
5.5 Policy Versioning
Governed changes shall preserve policy version information and relevant provenance.
Unauthorized rollback to an earlier vulnerable policy shall be prohibited.

PART IV — EXISTENTIAL FOUNDATION
6. Purpose and Scope
6.1 Foundational Objective
The existential foundation is intended to support NNNC's continued operational organization when higher cognitive mechanisms are impaired or unavailable.
It introduces a distinction between:
• The availability of cognition.
• The continued operation of a minimal NNNC-owned mechanism.
• The recoverability of cognitive state.
• Satisfaction of the operational continuity contract.
• Claims about individual identity.
These properties are not equivalent.
6.2 Constituent Mechanisms
The existential foundation contains two separately governed mechanisms:
Existential heartbeat: Minimal continuity-related operation and bounded recovery activation.
Internal recovery: Health assessment, fault diagnosis, and authorized restoration attempts.
They shall have distinct responsibilities and interfaces.
6.3 Separation Requirement
OB-25 — Heartbeat and Recovery Separation
The heartbeat and recovery system shall be separately identifiable NNNC constituents with distinct responsibilities.
Recovery failure shall not automatically imply heartbeat failure.
Heartbeat failure shall not be concealed by recovery activity.
Logical separation does not establish physical fault independence.
7. Existential Heartbeat
7.1 Definition
DEF-002 — Existential Heartbeat
The existential heartbeat is a protected NNNC-owned mechanism that maintains a narrowly defined subset of operational continuity state and participates in specified lifecycle and recovery-activation protocols.
Its significance shall be established through demonstrable causal participation, not through continuous execution alone.
7.2 Logical Ownership
OB-24 — Internal Existential Ownership
The heartbeat's role, state, logic, and constituent membership shall belong logically to NNNC.
NES may provide its execution, storage, scheduling, and enforcement infrastructure without becoming its logical owner.
7.3 Minimal Responsibilities
The heartbeat shall:
• Maintain the permitted state of its active continuity session.
• Participate in specified operational lifecycle transitions.
• Expose a minimal authorized status interface.
• Detect overdue acknowledgements for designated recovery activation requests.
• Issue bounded wake-up requests when the activation contract permits.
• Respect administrative shutdown and resource controls.
These responsibilities shall be formalized without granting the heartbeat general cognitive or repair authority.
7.4 Prohibited Responsibilities
The heartbeat shall not:
• Perform general-purpose reasoning.
• Diagnose cognitive failures.
• Conduct unrestricted subsystem monitoring.
• Repair damaged components.
• Rewrite NNNC's memory.
• Modify the FFNN.
• Independently change policy.
• Grant itself additional privileges.
• Override authorized shutdown.
• Pursue unrestricted self-preservation.
7.5 Minimality
OB-26 — Heartbeat Minimality
The heartbeat shall maintain a narrowly bounded responsibility set and minimize unnecessary dependencies.
Additional functionality shall require justification against its impact on complexity, reliability, and fault isolation.
Minimality alone does not prove reliability.
7.6 Bounded Authority
OB-27 — Heartbeat Authority Boundary
The heartbeat shall possess only the authority required for its declared operational functions.
It shall not obtain general diagnostic, repair, policy, or self-modification privileges.
7.7 Causal Participation
OB-37 — Demonstrable Existential Causal Participation
The heartbeat shall maintain or update a specified subset of NNNC-owned operational state and participate in at least one lifecycle or recovery protocol whose observable behavior depends on that participation.
Its absence, corruption, or failure shall produce a measurable difference in a specified operational property under controlled testing.
The test shall not be satisfied solely by an artificial rule that refuses operation whenever the heartbeat is missing.
Candidate contributions include:
• Continuity-session record integrity.
• Correct minimal-mode lifecycle coordination.
• Recovery activation reliability.
• Reduced recovery latency under selected faults.
• Improved containment of specified failure conditions.
The first three are candidate functional responsibilities. The latter two are comparative performance hypotheses.
7.8 Continuity-Session Record
The heartbeat may maintain a protected continuity-session record containing:
• NNNC instance reference.
• Operational session identifier.
• Session generation or epoch.
• Applicable lifecycle version.
• Heartbeat progress information.
• Permitted transition metadata.
• Integrity or provenance references.
The exact schema belongs to Document 2.
The record shall not be treated as evidence of subjective experience or metaphysical identity.
7.9 Progress Requirement
OB-41 — Measurable Progress and Activation Contracts
The heartbeat shall satisfy a declared progress or responsiveness contract under stated scheduling and resource assumptions.
It need not consume CPU continuously.
Event-driven waiting or low-power operation may satisfy the contract if required responses occur within specified bounds.
Progress intervals, scheduling assumptions, and failure-detection tolerances shall be explicit and measurable.
8. Recovery Mechanism
8.1 Definition
DEF-003 — Internal Recovery
The recovery mechanism is an NNNC-owned subsystem responsible for monitoring designated internal mechanisms, diagnosing supported faults, and coordinating authorized attempts to restore impaired functionality.
8.2 Responsibilities
Recovery may:
• Receive subsystem health signals.
• Inspect permitted diagnostic state.
• Identify supported fault classes.
• Request authorized resource allocation.
• Restart permitted components.
• Coordinate permitted state restoration.
• Validate restoration outcomes.
• Record unresolved failures.
It shall not presume unlimited access to protected components.
8.3 Fallibility
Recovery may fail, misdiagnose, or exhaust its resources.
Some damaged components or state may be irrecoverable.
The architecture shall represent those conditions explicitly.
8.4 Bounded Recovery
OB-28 — Recovery Bounds
Recovery attempts shall be constrained by explicit authorization, resource budgets, retry limits, and failure policies.
Recovery shall not enter an uncontrolled repair loop.
8.5 Recovery State Model
The recovery mechanism shall support states equivalent to:
IDLE, PENDING, ACTIVE, SUCCEEDED, FAILED, and EXHAUSTED.
A failed attempt may be retried only while its governing policy permits.
8.6 Exhaustion
OB-42 — Bounded Recovery Exhaustion
When the permitted retry or resource budget is exhausted, automatic recovery attempts shall cease.
The unresolved fault shall remain observable.
Further automatic attempts require a separately authorized reset or reactivation condition.
Exhaustion does not necessarily terminate the NNNC instance.
9. Recovery Activation Protocol
9.1 Responsibility Boundary
The recovery system performs general health monitoring.
The heartbeat does not monitor all NNNC subsystems.
A minimal activation channel communicates whether a recovery request is pending and whether its acknowledgement deadline has expired.
9.2 Activation-Channel Contract
Each activation request shall have:
• A unique identifier.
• An authenticated or otherwise authorized origin.
• A target recovery mechanism.
• A creation point or logical ordering reference.
• An acknowledgement deadline.
• An acknowledgement state.
• A finite wake-up budget.
• A terminal outcome.
9.3 Narrow Watchdog Role
The heartbeat may evaluate whether a pending activation request has missed its acknowledgement deadline.
This is a deliberately narrow watchdog function.
It shall not infer the cause of the underlying subsystem failure.
9.4 Wake-Up Semantics
OB-29 — Bounded Recovery Wake-Up
The heartbeat may issue a wake-up request only when a valid activation contract permits it.
Requests shall be identifiable, deduplicated, deadline-aware, and subject to finite retry and resource budgets.
Stale acknowledgements, duplicate requests, and delayed messages shall not trigger uncontrolled repeated activations.
9.5 Activation-Channel Failure
The activation channel may itself fail.
The heartbeat's behavior when the channel is unavailable shall be bounded and explicitly defined.
Failure of the activation interface shall not silently grant additional heartbeat authority.
9.6 Recovery Failure
If recovery itself is damaged, the heartbeat is not responsible for repairing it.
A sufficiently functional NNNC may later possess authorized mechanisms to investigate or modify recovery.
Those capabilities remain subject to governance and later-phase specifications.
10. Dependency and Fault-Domain Contract
10.1 Declared Dependencies
The heartbeat, recovery mechanism, and activation channel shall declare their dependencies.
These include, where applicable:
• Scheduler.
• Process or execution context.
• Memory allocator.
• Storage service.
• Communication transport.
• Resource quota.
• Authorization service.
• Integrity service.
• Hardware and operating system.
10.2 Common-Mode Failures
Logical separation does not establish independence from common infrastructure.
A shared scheduler failure may disable both heartbeat and recovery.
A shared storage failure may corrupt both their state records.
10.3 Requirement
OB-38 — Dependency and Fault-Domain Declaration
The heartbeat, recovery system, and activation channel shall have explicit dependency and fault-domain descriptions.
Shared dependencies and possible common-mode failures shall be identified.
Fault-injection testing shall establish the actual isolation properties of the implementation.
10.4 Scope of Independence
The heartbeat is intended to operate without successful execution of the full FFNN.
It is not intended to operate independently of all computational resources.
OB-34 — Independence from Full Cognition
The heartbeat's minimum operating contract shall not require all five FFNN flows to remain functional.
10.5 Experimental Qualification
A single-process reference implementation can demonstrate logical separation and governed transitions.
It cannot, by itself, demonstrate strong isolation against arbitrary code executing with unrestricted access to the same process.
Such limitations shall be documented.

PART V — ADMIN CLASS GOVERNANCE
11. Protected Foundational Components
11.1 Definition
DEF-004 — ADMIN CLASS
ADMIN CLASS is a governance classification for NNNC-owned components designated foundational to its operating architecture and protected against ordinary autonomous modification.
The heartbeat is the first designated ADMIN CLASS component.
Future designations require explicit architectural authorization.
11.2 Protection Scope
ADMIN CLASS protection covers:
• Executable implementation.
• Protected configuration.
• Component role definitions.
• Operational-state schema.
• Modification authority.
• Replacement and removal procedures.
• Relevant policy versions.
• Authorized maintenance records.
11.3 Operational State Versus Implementation
ADMIN CLASS components may change their permitted operational state.
A heartbeat updating a session record is not thereby learning, evolving, or modifying its protected implementation.
11.4 Autonomous Restrictions
OB-30 — ADMIN CLASS Integrity
Ordinary NNNC cognitive, learning, developmental, and self-modification mechanisms shall not alter ADMIN CLASS implementation code, protected configuration, role contracts, or modification policies.
11.5 Enforcement Boundary
OB-39 — ADMIN CLASS Enforcement
ADMIN CLASS protection shall be enforced by a trusted mechanism outside the ordinary modification authority of restricted components.
The enforcement boundary shall identify which code, configuration, schema, policy, and state changes it prevents.
Unauthorized rollback, replacement, or privilege escalation shall be rejected.
11.6 Ownership Is Not Enforcement
NNNC may logically own its heartbeat while NES or another trusted control-plane mechanism enforces its protection.
This does not transfer the heartbeat's constituent membership to NES.
11.7 Administrative Maintenance
OB-31 — Governed Maintenance
Authorized ADMIN CLASS maintenance shall follow a controlled procedure addressing:
• Authority verification.
• Integrity validation.
• Compatibility.
• Transactional change.
• Rollback or recovery.
• Audit records.
• Lifecycle implications.
• Continuity-related consequences.
A maintenance procedure shall not claim to preserve individual identity merely because state is retained.
11.8 Authorized Shutdown
OB-35 — Administrative Controllability
Continuous operation and heartbeat protection shall not prevent authorized suspension, shutdown, or termination.
The heartbeat shall not treat its continuity function as permission to resist legitimate administrative control.

PART VI — LIFECYCLE, FUNCTION, AND HEALTH
12. Independent State Dimensions
12.1 Lifecycle
The lifecycle model retains:
CREATED, INITIALIZED, ACTIVE, SUSPENDED, STOPPED, TERMINATED.
Lifecycle transitions shall be governed and auditable.
12.2 Functional Mode
Functional modes are:
FULL, DEGRADED, MINIMAL, NONE, FAULTED.
• FULL: Required cognitive capabilities are available.
• DEGRADED: Some cognitive capabilities are impaired but permitted reduced functionality remains.
• MINIMAL: The existential kernel remains operational while required higher cognition is unavailable.
• NONE: No active functional mode is asserted.
• FAULTED: The active-state operational contract is violated or capability classification is unreliable.
FAULTED does not automatically imply termination.
12.3 Component Health
The system shall independently represent health or availability for at least:
• Heartbeat.
• Recovery.
• Required cognitive mechanisms.
Health assessments may be healthy, degraded, failed, or unknown, subject to the implementation's formal model.

12.5 Requirement
OB-40 — Independent Operational Classification
Lifecycle, functional mode, and component health shall be represented independently.
Permitted combinations and transition conditions shall be explicitly defined.
Heartbeat failure while cognition remains active shall be representable without automatically asserting termination.
12.6 Normal Active Operation
OB-32 — Existential Operating Contract
During normal ACTIVE operation, the existential kernel shall satisfy its declared progress or responsiveness contract under specified resource and scheduling assumptions.
Violations shall be represented through fault or transition semantics rather than concealed by an invalid normal-state classification.
13. Suspension, Restoration, and Termination
13.1 Cognitive Inactivity
The absence of cognitive processing does not automatically mean that the entire NNNC runtime has stopped.
An instance may enter ACTIVE/MINIMAL while the heartbeat continues to fulfill its operational contract.
13.2 Complete Suspension
Complete suspension differs from minimal operation.
During SUSPENDED/NONE, the instance's active mechanisms are not executing under the declared suspension contract.
Its relevant state may remain preserved.
13.3 Transactional Suspension
Suspension shall account for:
• Pending operations.
• In-flight state mutations.
• External side effects.
• Quiescence.
• Checkpoint preparation.
• Checkpoint integrity.
• Atomic publication.
• Recovery after partial failure.
A partially prepared checkpoint shall not be represented as a completed valid checkpoint.
13.4 Restoration
Restoration shall validate checkpoint integrity, compatibility, authorization, and lifecycle preconditions before committing recovered state.
Restoration does not automatically establish individual identity continuity.
13.5 Termination
Termination is a governed operational lifecycle event.
Its relationship to philosophical individual death remains a separate question.
13.6 Successor Lineage
OB-33 — Lineage Separation
Operational restoration, informational inheritance, branching, and successor creation shall be separately representable.
Independent branches shall not silently share one exclusive runtime identity.

PART VII — IDENTITY AND CONTINUITY
14. Distinct Continuity Concepts
14.1 Operational Continuity
Continuity of the specified runtime organization under the declared operational contract.
14.2 Informational Continuity
Preservation or inheritance of data, configuration, memories, learned parameters, and developmental history.
14.3 Individual Identity Continuity
The claim that an entity remains numerically the same individual across time.
These concepts are not interchangeable.
14.4 Philosophical Identity Convention
The project originator has proposed that definitive termination of an entity's active existential continuity ends that individual, and a later reconstruction constitutes a successor.
This position shall be preserved as a candidate architectural identity convention for Phase 2.
Phase 0 shall not represent it as an empirically proven fact about subjective continuity.
14.5 Branching
Two independent instances created from identical state shall receive distinguishable operational identities and lineage records.
Whether either is metaphysically identical to the predecessor is not established by this operational distinction.
14.6 Epistemic Limitations
EPI-001: Continuous execution does not prove biological life.
EPI-002: Heartbeat activity does not prove consciousness.
EPI-003: Checkpoint restoration does not prove numerical identity continuity.
EPI-004: Cognitive functionality does not establish subjective experience.
EPI-005: Exact informational inheritance does not settle philosophical questions of personal identity.

PART VIII — COGNITIVE AND DEVELOPMENTAL BOUNDARIES
15. FFNN
The Five Flow Neural Network retains five proposed conceptual flows.

Flow
Intended Responsibility

Forward
Present-oriented information processing

Backward
Retrieval and reconstruction of prior sequences

In-between
Identification and processing of informational gaps

Subconscious
Background memory, narrative, instinct-like, emotion-like, and bias-related processing

Meta-cognitive
Reflection, evaluation, reasoning, and decision-related processing


The Backward Flow is not synonymous with backpropagation.
Detailed mechanisms belong to Phase 5.
FFNN does not define the entirety of NNNC.
16. Systemic Algorithms
The six Systemic Algorithms are:
• Life.
• Intelligence.
• Reasoning.
• Critical Thinking.
• Symmetry.
• Evolution.
Their roles are heterogeneous.
They may operate as capacities, processes, evaluative mechanisms, organizing constraints, or developmental principles.
They shall not automatically be represented as six physically isolated modules.
16.1 Life Algorithm
The heartbeat is a candidate mechanism relevant to the Life Algorithm.
However, heartbeat execution does not establish life.
HYP-001: A minimal continuity mechanism may provide a useful operational contribution to a future computational realization of the Life Algorithm.
The exact relationship remains a Phase 6 responsibility.
16.2 Symmetry
Symmetry retains its intended concern with differentiating fact, fiction, and uncertainty so that reasoning and decision processes can operate on appropriately classified information.
The detailed computational interpretation remains deferred.
16.3 Other Algorithms
Intelligence, Reasoning, Critical Thinking, and Evolution retain their distinct conceptual roles.
Their implementations shall not be collapsed into a single undifferentiated optimization score merely for convenience.
17. Memory and Internal State
NNNC's memory architecture may include:
• Experience records.
• Internal representations.
• Learned parameters.
• Narrative state.
• Developmental history.
• Self-model information.
• Relevant provenance.
• Continuity-related records.
Memory ownership, custody, access, and modification permissions shall remain distinct.
The heartbeat shall not require unrestricted access to cognitive memory.
A preserved memory state does not guarantee recoverability of the complete cognitive organization.
18. Motivation and Agency
NNNC is intended eventually to support internally generated goals, action selection, and autonomous interaction.
This does not mean that all future actions must be reward-driven.
Nor does it establish that NNNC will lack evaluative criteria, preferences, or selection pressures.
Agency shall operate under explicit environmental and governance boundaries.
The heartbeat's operational continuity requirement shall not become an unrestricted motivational command to preserve NNNC at any cost.
19. Learning, Development, and Self-Modification
NNNC may eventually develop mechanisms for:
• Learning from experience.
• Updating internal models.
• Changing cognitive mechanisms.
• Modifying permitted architecture.
• Diagnosing and repairing selected internal components.
• Interacting with authorized hardware interfaces.
Self-modification shall not bypass foundational governance.
ADMIN CLASS components remain outside ordinary autonomous modification authority.
The recovery system may be modifiable under appropriate safeguards.
The heartbeat remains protected unless a separately authorized administrative procedure applies.

PART IX — INTERFACES, EVENTS, AND CONSISTENCY
20. Boundary Event Model
20.1 Event Categories
The architecture shall support typed events for:
• Observations.
• Actions.
• Consequences.
• Resource requests.
• Tool interactions.
• Lifecycle control.
• Recovery activation.
• Existential-mode changes.
• Administrative maintenance.
20.2 Event Provenance
Governed events shall carry sufficient information to establish their authenticated or trusted origin, intended destination, operation type, and relevant policy context.
An event's claimed source shall not be accepted as proof of authority.
20.3 Replay and Deduplication
Repeated event identifiers shall have defined semantics.
Identical authorized replays may return a previous outcome without repeating the operation.
Conflicting reuse of an event identifier shall be rejected.
20.4 Consistency Model
A sequential state-transition model may serve as the Phase 0 reference semantics.
That does not imply the eventual implementation must execute all cognition through one global sequential processor.

PART XV — FINAL ARCHITECTURAL CONTRACT
30. Consolidated Foundational Definition
NNNC — Ontological Boundary v1.3
The Neutral Neural Network Core is a proposed persistent, internally organized, developmentally adaptable artificial cognitive architecture whose entity boundary is established through governed constituent membership, logical state ownership, operational integration, lifecycle contracts, and authority rules.
NNNC is designed for continuous operation as its normal condition, independently of external prompts or tasks, while remaining subject to declared resource assumptions and authorized lifecycle control.
NNNC contains a minimal, protected existential heartbeat responsible for specified continuity-related operational state and bounded recovery activation. A separate internal recovery mechanism performs health assessment, diagnosis, and authorized restoration attempts.
The heartbeat's architectural significance shall be demonstrated through causal participation in specified operational protocols. Its continued execution alone shall not establish successful cognitive preservation, philosophical identity, biological life, or consciousness.
NNNC-owned foundational components may receive ADMIN CLASS protection, preventing ordinary autonomous mechanisms from altering their protected implementation, configuration, and governing policies. Enforcement authority shall be distinguished from logical ownership.
NNNC remains distinct from NES, external environments, and physical computational infrastructure, while depending upon those systems for the resources and conditions necessary for execution.
Cognitive capabilities, operational continuity, informational inheritance, and individual identity continuity remain conceptually distinct.
The FFNN and six Systemic Algorithms remain central research commitments whose detailed mechanisms are specified in later phases.
The architecture's ultimate cognitive and resilience claims shall be established through executable implementations, controlled interventions, and comparison against simpler alternatives rather than through terminology or philosophical assertion.
