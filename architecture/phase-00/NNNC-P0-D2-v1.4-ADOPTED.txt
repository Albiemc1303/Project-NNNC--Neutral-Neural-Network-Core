PART I — RECOVERED v1.3 BASE PROVISIONS (OPERATIVE SUBJECT TO EXPLICIT AMENDMENTS)

0. Constitution and scope

0.1 Purpose
This document defines the executable reference semantics of the Phase 0 NNNC boundary kernel.
It formalizes:
1. Entity registration, constituent membership, and operational classification.
2. Ownership, custody, authorization, and protected state.
3. The existential heartbeat and its minimal causal obligations.
4. Recovery cycles and independent activation requests.
5. Monotonic-time semantics and bounded retries.
6. ADMIN CLASS enforcement and delegated authority.
7. Lifecycle, functional mode, and health evidence.
8. Continuity sessions, fencing, branching, and restoration.
9. Fault containment, forced suspension, and checkpoint integrity.
10. Safety, liveness, recoverability, conformance, and experimental verification.
The reference model is a deterministic, sequential control-plane specification. A distributed implementation must refine its safety properties without assuming that one local heartbeat implies globally exclusive authority.

0.2 Epistemic constraints
No successful implementation of this document establishes consciousness, subjective experience, biological life, general intelligence, or metaphysical identity continuity.
The existential heartbeat is an architectural hypothesis whose causal function must be demonstrated.
Its superiority over conventional supervision is not assumed.

0.3 Normative notation
- SHALL: required for the relevant conformance profile.
- SHALL NOT: prohibited.
- SHOULD: recommended, with justified deviations permitted.
- MAY: optional.
- Invariant: property required of every committed valid state.
- Assumption: environmental condition necessary for a claimed guarantee.
- Hypothesis: empirically testable proposition, not an established result.

0.4 Formal domains
Let:
\mathcal N: NNNC instance identifiers
\mathcal C: component identifiers
\mathcal P: authenticated principals
\mathcal X: registered state objects
\mathcal V: governed events
\mathcal Q: recovery-cycle identifiers
\mathcal U: activation-request identifiers
\mathcal S: execution-session identifiers
\mathcal F: fault-domain identifiers
Identifiers are opaque, canonical, and unique within their declared namespace.

0.5 Separate logical and elapsed time
Committed transition order is k in Nat.
Trusted monotonic elapsed time is tau in R_{>=0}.
The two are not interchangeable.
The reference transition function is:
(Sigma_{k+1},o_k) = delta(Sigma_k,e_k,tau_k)
where o_k is an observable result.
A rejected command may leave the domain state unchanged; it need not create a new committed domain-state version.
For successive processed events: tau_{k+1} >= tau_k.
The clock value is supplied by a trusted clock interface, not an untrusted event payload.
Timer delivery
Elapsed time does not itself execute a transition.
A trusted timer source SHALL deliver a "TIMER_EXPIRED" event when an applicable deadline is reached, subject to its scheduling and failure assumptions.
Build 2 SHALL support an injectable deterministic virtual monotonic clock.
The test harness SHALL distinguish:
- Advancing virtual time.
- Delivering a timer event.
- Committing a state transition.
Commit-time rule
Time-dependent permissions and request status SHALL be revalidated at the operation's linearization or commit point.
A wake-up authorized before acknowledgement SHALL NOT commit after an acknowledgement invalidates its preconditions.

1. Global reference state

1.1 State tuple
The Phase 0 state remains:
Sigma_k=(R,D,F,O,K,A,L,B,H,J,M,T,X,Y,Z,G,W)_k
Field | Responsibility
R | Instance and component registry
D | Declared membership
F | Roles and component contracts
O | Canonical ownership
K | Custody and replica relationships
A | Authorization and governance
L | Lifecycle
B | Budgets and reservations
H | Execution and deployment mapping
J | Committed-event provenance references
M | Metadata, versions, and integrity
T | Pending transactions
X | Heartbeat runtime state
Y | Recovery cycles and activation requests
Z | Health evidence and derived functional mode
G | Canonical continuity sessions and lineage
W | Dependencies and fault domains
A partition is a logical responsibility, not necessarily a separate physical database.

1.2 Canonical versus derived state
Every authoritative fact SHALL have one canonical source.
Derived views SHALL either be computed from canonical state or carry a version reference that permits consistency validation.
Duplicated authoritative fields are prohibited unless an explicit synchronization invariant and atomic update rule exist.

1.3 Domain state and audit state
Define: Omega_k=(Sigma_k,mathcal A_k)
where mathcal A_k is a separately append-only audit stream.
A rejected command: Sigma_{k+1}=Sigma_k
may append a rejection record to mathcal A.
An accepted failure event may legitimately update recovery or health state.
A rejection SHALL NOT implicitly mutate protected operational state.

1.4 Validity
Define: Valid(Sigma)= conjunction_{i in I_applicable} I_i(Sigma)
where the applicable invariants depend on the conformance profile and state.
This is a specification obligation, not a theorem already proved.

2. Instance classification

2.1 Declaration
Declared(n,Sigma)
holds when n has a valid registration, constituent manifest, and governance metadata.
Declaration does not imply execution.

2.2 Execution activity
ExecutionActive(n,Sigma)
holds when the instance is in lifecycle "ACTIVE" and at least one authorized constituent is executing or eligible to execute under its runtime contract.
This predicate may be true during a fault.

2.3 Operational conformance
OperationalConformant(n,Sigma,E)
holds when all requirements of the selected operational profile are satisfied by state and valid evidence E.
An "ACTIVE/FAULTED" instance may be execution-active but not operationally conformant.

2.4 Cognitive availability
CognitiveAvailable(n,Sigma,E)
describes whether the required cognitive capability contract is currently satisfied.
It does not imply that the heartbeat or recovery is healthy.

2.5 Cognitive capability conformance
CognitiveCapabilityConformant(n,mathcal T)
means that the cognitive subsystem satisfies a defined test suite mathcal T.
This may hold independently of whole-instance operational conformance.
A separate whole-instance predicate may require both:
WholeInstanceConformant = OperationalConformant AND CognitiveCapabilityConformant
The former implication that cognitive conformance necessarily implies operational conformance is therefore withdrawn unless the whole-instance definition is explicitly used.

2.6 Examples
Condition | Execution active | Operationally conformant
Normal full operation | Yes | Yes
Valid minimal operation | Yes | Yes, under minimal profile
Heartbeat failed, cognition active | Yes | No
Suspended with valid checkpoint | No | Not currently operating
Terminated | No | No

3. Membership, ownership, custody, and authority

3.1 Membership
D:mathcal C -> mathcal N union {bottom}
A component has at most one canonical declared NNNC membership.

3.2 Ownership
O:mathcal X -> mathcal N union mathcal P union {bottom}
Protected state objects SHALL have a non-null canonical owner.

3.3 Custody multiplicity
To preserve support for replication, custody is a relation:
K subseteq mathcal X x mathcal K
where mathcal K is the set of custodians.
An object MAY have multiple authorized custodians.
A designated authoritative custodian or consensus-governed custody group SHALL be identified when required for writes.
Custody does not imply ownership.

3.4 Heartbeat instance binding
For heartbeat h_n: D(h_n)=n
and its governed operations SHALL be scoped to the owning instance n.
Its continuity-session reference SHALL resolve to a session belonging to n.
The heartbeat SHALL NOT issue authorized operations for unrelated NNNC instances.
This is a causal and state-binding requirement, not merely a registry label.
Cryptographic binding MAY strengthen enforcement but is not required for the abstract ownership definition.

3.5 Constitutive significance
Membership alone does not prove causal significance.
The heartbeat's operational contribution SHALL be evaluated through specified lifecycle and recovery protocols.

4. Authorization and ADMIN CLASS

4.1 Authorization context
Every protected operation carries a trusted authorization context:
AuthCtx=(originator,executor,delegation,scope,policyVersion)
The originator and executor may differ.
The delegation field identifies an authenticated, bounded authorization chain.

4.2 Authorization predicate
May(AuthCtx,operation,target,Sigma)
holds only when:
1. The originator is authenticated.
2. The executor is authenticated.
3. The executor is permitted to perform the operation.
4. Every delegation step is valid.
5. Delegation scope covers the operation and target.
6. The governing policy version is valid.
7. No applicable revocation has committed.
8. ADMIN CLASS restrictions are satisfied.
An executor's privileges alone are insufficient.

4.3 Confused-deputy prevention
A privileged service SHALL NOT use its authority to perform an ADMIN CLASS modification merely because an ordinary cognitive component requested it.
Nested calls and callbacks SHALL preserve the authenticated origin and delegation context.
An ordinary cognitive principal cannot convert a maintenance proposal into administrative authorization.

4.4 Prior-policy evaluation
A proposed policy modification SHALL be authorized under the committed policy preceding that modification.
Self-granting policy changes are prohibited.

4.5 ADMIN CLASS protection
For ADMIN CLASS component c, protected fields include:
P_c={code,configuration,role,schema,policy,version}
Ordinary autonomous principals SHALL NOT modify these fields.
Permitted heartbeat operational-state updates SHALL use separate, narrowly authorized transitions.

4.6 Operational-state protection
Counters, deadlines, session references, and other mutable heartbeat fields SHALL NOT be arbitrarily reset by ordinary callers.
Permitted updates SHALL be validated against the heartbeat state machine.

4.7 Trusted enforcement boundary
The implementation SHALL identify the trusted computing base enforcing these rules.
Build 2 MAY use immutable in-process state and a trusted transition kernel.
Such an implementation SHALL NOT claim isolation against arbitrary malicious code with unrestricted access to the same process.

4.8 Administrative maintenance
A maintenance transaction SHALL validate:
- Originator and executor authority.
- Scope and delegation.
- Protected component identity.
- Implementation and schema compatibility.
- Policy version.
- Required integrity evidence.
- Atomic commit or rollback.
- Lifecycle consequences.
Unauthorized rollback is prohibited.

5. Lifecycle and functional mode

5.1 Lifecycle
L(n) in {CREATED,INITIALIZED,ACTIVE,SUSPENDED,STOPPED,TERMINATED}

5.2 Functional mode
Mode(n) in {FULL,DEGRADED,MINIMAL,NONE,FAULTED}

5.3 Component health
For Build 2: Health(n)=(h_H,h_R,h_C)
Each component has a health assessment in {HEALTHY,DEGRADED,FAILED,UNKNOWN}.
Health is an evidence-backed assessment, not necessarily the actual underlying condition.

5.4 Health observation
A health observation contains:
obs=(component,value,observedAt,expiresAt,source,version)
An expired observation SHALL evaluate to "UNKNOWN" unless replaced by fresh trusted evidence.
A component's own unverified claim of health SHALL NOT automatically establish conformance.

5.5 Lifecycle–mode compatibility
Lifecycle | Permitted mode
CREATED | NONE
INITIALIZED | NONE
ACTIVE | FULL, DEGRADED, MINIMAL, FAULTED
SUSPENDED | NONE
STOPPED | NONE
TERMINATED | NONE
The mode is a derived state view. It SHALL NOT be independently writable through ordinary commands.

5.6 Deterministic Build 2 mode derivation
For lifecycle l, heartbeat health h_H, recovery health h_R, and cognition health h_C, define the following ordered decision function.
Rule 1 — Non-active gate
If l != ACTIVE then Mode=NONE.
Rule 2 — Existential or evidence fault
If h_H in {FAILED,UNKNOWN} or h_C=UNKNOWN then Mode=FAULTED.
Rule 3 — Minimal operation
If h_C=FAILED and heartbeat health is acceptable under the minimal profile, then Mode=MINIMAL.
For Build 2, acceptable heartbeat health means "HEALTHY" or a separately validated "DEGRADED" state that still satisfies the minimal heartbeat contract.
Rule 4 — Degraded operation
If h_C=DEGRADED or h_H=DEGRADED or h_R in {DEGRADED,FAILED,UNKNOWN} then Mode=DEGRADED.
Rule 5 — Full operation
Otherwise Mode=FULL provided all required capability contracts are satisfied.
For the Build 2 placeholder, the health values are defined to encode the corresponding capability assessments.
Later phases SHALL replace this simplification with explicit capability predicates.

5.7 Totality
The ordered rules SHALL return exactly one mode for every combination of the 64 possible three-component health vectors when lifecycle is "ACTIVE".

5.8 Faulted-state exit
An "ACTIVE/FAULTED" instance SHALL have governed paths to:
1. Authorized repair or ADMIN CLASS maintenance.
2. Forced or ordinary suspension.
3. Controlled stop.
4. Authorized termination.
A heartbeat failure SHALL NOT automatically terminate the instance.
The control plane MAY perform authorized maintenance while the heartbeat is unavailable.

6. Canonical continuity sessions

6.1 Single authoritative record
Continuity-session state is canonical in G, not duplicated independently in X.
Define G(n)=(sessionId,epoch,status,parent,history,authority)
where:
- "sessionId" identifies the execution session.
- "epoch" is a monotonic fencing generation.
- "status" records session status.
- "parent" identifies predecessor lineage, if applicable.
- "history" references committed transitions.
- "authority" identifies the current session-authority mechanism.

6.2 Heartbeat reference
Heartbeat state contains a reference to the canonical session:
X(n).sessionRef -> G(n)
It SHALL NOT maintain an independently authoritative copy of "sessionId" or "epoch".

6.3 Exclusive primary authority
For each NNNC instance and real execution interval:
|ValidPrimaryAuthorities(n,tau)| <= 1
A distributed implementation SHALL use an appropriate fencing or consensus mechanism to enforce this property.

6.4 Fencing tokens
Protected writes SHALL carry a valid session fencing token.
A stale token SHALL NOT authorize a protected commit.
A partition that cannot establish current authority SHALL cease protected writes under the old session authority.
Local heartbeat execution may continue without global primary authority, but it SHALL NOT be treated as proof of exclusive session control.

6.5 Epoch authority
The trusted session-management control plane governs epoch advancement.
The heartbeat may participate in the lifecycle protocol but is not the sole authority to increment epochs.
This preserves administrative recovery paths when the heartbeat fails.

6.6 Branching
An independent restored branch SHALL receive a new execution-session identifier before becoming active.
It may retain the source session identifier as a lineage reference.

6.7 Restart
Restarting the original registered instance SHALL invalidate stale execution attempts through a new fencing generation or equivalent protected authority transition.

6.8 Identity limitation
Session continuity and lineage records are operational constructs.
They do not establish metaphysical numerical identity.

7. Existential heartbeat

7.1 Heartbeat state
Define X(n)=(sessionRef,q,lastProgress,version,activationBudget,integrityRef)
where q in {READY,RUNNING,WAITING,FAULTED,HALTED}.
The canonical session ID and epoch remain in G.

7.2 Permitted transitions
Current | Event | Next | Condition
READY | HB_START | RUNNING | Authorized activation
RUNNING | HB_WAIT | WAITING | No required immediate work
WAITING | HB_PROGRESS | RUNNING | Trusted progress event
RUNNING | HB_PROGRESS | RUNNING | Valid progress update
RUNNING/WAITING | HB_FAULT | FAULTED | Trusted fault evidence
FAULTED | ADMIN_RESTORE | READY | Authorized maintenance
READY/RUNNING/WAITING/FAULTED | HB_HALT | HALTED | Authorized lifecycle control
Other transitions are rejected unless explicitly added through a governed revision.

7.3 Progress contract
Let Delta_H be the declared heartbeat progress or response bound.
Under stated scheduler and resource assumptions, the heartbeat SHALL produce required progress evidence or respond to designated events within Delta_H.
Idle waiting may be compliant.
An unnecessary busy loop is not required.

7.4 Progress evidence
A progress record SHALL identify:
- The relevant session reference.
- Trusted observation time.
- Heartbeat state version.
- Relevant processed event or permitted idle-response evidence.
- Integrity or provenance reference.
A mere counter increment is insufficient evidence of useful causal participation.

7.5 Causal role
The heartbeat SHALL participate in:
1. Continuity-session operational observation.
2. Valid minimal-mode support.
3. Bounded recovery activation when an authorized request is overdue.
Its contribution SHALL be tested through controlled intervention and mechanism-matched ablation.

7.6 Authority limitations
The heartbeat SHALL NOT diagnose arbitrary cognitive failures, repair protected components, grant itself privileges, or override authorized shutdown.

8. Recovery cycles

8.1 Recovery state
For recovery cycle q:
Y^rec(q)=(id,instance,state,attempts,maxAttempts,resourceUsed,resourceLimit,faultRef,history)
where state in {IDLE,PENDING,ACTIVE,SUCCEEDED,FAILED,EXHAUSTED}.

8.2 Recovery transition table
Current | Event | Next | Guard
IDLE | RECOVERY_REQUEST | PENDING | Authorized request
PENDING | ATTEMPT_ADMIT | ACTIVE | Budget available
ACTIVE | ATTEMPT_SUCCESS | SUCCEEDED | Restoration validated
ACTIVE | ATTEMPT_FAILURE | FAILED | Attempt failed
ACTIVE | ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT | FAILED | Trusted timeout
FAILED | RETRY_REQUEST | PENDING | Attempts and resources remain
FAILED | EXHAUST | EXHAUSTED | No permitted retry remains
PENDING | EXHAUST | EXHAUSTED | Budget or policy prevents attempt
SUCCEEDED | CLOSE_CYCLE | IDLE | Successful cycle committed
EXHAUSTED | ADMIN_RESET | IDLE | Separately authorized reset
A new recovery cycle SHALL use a new cycle identifier.

8.3 Atomic attempt admission
Admission of an attempt SHALL atomically reserve or consume its configured attempt allowance and required resource budget.
A crash or timeout SHALL NOT restore a consumed attempt automatically.

8.4 Attempt accounting
0 <= attempts <= maxAttempts.
An attempt is counted when admitted, not only when it completes.
Resource consumption SHALL be accounted according to a declared reservation and settlement policy.

8.5 Exhaustion semantics
If no further attempt is permitted, an authorized failure or budget-evaluation event SHALL transition the cycle to "EXHAUSTED".
The system SHALL NOT depend on attempting an unauthorized (maxAttempts+1)-th operation to discover exhaustion.

8.6 Terminal exhaustion
An "EXHAUSTED" cycle SHALL NOT transition directly to "ACTIVE".
A separately authorized reset may close or replace the cycle while preserving historical audit records.

8.7 Recovery outcome
"SUCCEEDED" means the specified restoration contract was validated.
It does not imply restoration of all cognitive capabilities or individual identity continuity.

9. Independent recovery activation

9.1 Activation producers
A recovery activation request may originate from:
- An authorized subsystem fault reporter.
- A trusted runtime fault trap.
- An authorized administrative controller.
- Another explicitly registered fault source.
The activation pathway SHALL NOT require recovery itself to execute successfully in order to create a request.

9.2 Activation request state
For activation request a:
Y^act(a)=(id,cycleId,instance,target,createdAt,nextDeadline,wakeCount,maxWakes,status,generation)
where status in {AWAITING_ACK,ACKNOWLEDGED,COMPLETED,FAILED,EXHAUSTED}.
Wake-up attempts are events and counters, not separate terminal statuses.

9.3 Overdue predicate
Overdue(a,tau) iff status(a)=AWAITING_ACK AND tau>=nextDeadline(a).

9.4 Wake-up admission
A wake-up may commit only if:
Overdue(a,tau) AND wakeCount(a)<maxWakes(a) AND ValidAuthority(a) AND ValidSession(a) AND ResourcesAvailable(a).
All guards are rechecked at commit.

9.5 Wake-up transition
A successful wake-up admission atomically:
1. Records a uniquely identified wake-up attempt.
2. Increments "wakeCount".
3. Reserves its resource allowance.
4. Sets "nextDeadline" to a later trusted monotonic time.
5. Preserves "AWAITING_ACK" status.
Thus additional bounded wake-ups remain reachable.

9.6 Acknowledgement
An acknowledgement SHALL identify:
- Activation request ID.
- Recovery cycle ID.
- Session generation.
- Authorized recovery endpoint.
- Corresponding attempt or request version.
Stale or mismatched acknowledgements SHALL NOT clear a current request.

9.7 Exhaustion
After the final permitted wake-up, the request remains eligible for acknowledgement until its final deadline.
If that deadline expires without valid acknowledgement, the request transitions to "EXHAUSTED".
No further automatic wake-ups occur.

9.8 Channel failure
If the activation channel becomes unavailable, the heartbeat SHALL NOT infer permission for unlimited retries.
The implementation SHALL define a bounded channel-failure outcome.
No recovery guarantee is asserted if the trusted activation channel and all authorized request producers are unavailable.

9.9 Duplicate handling
Identical authenticated request replays are idempotent.
Conflicting reuse of an activation request identifier is rejected.
A duplicated acknowledgement cannot consume or restore wake-up budget.

10. Fault domains and dependencies

10.1 Dependency sets
Let D_H,D_R,D_A subseteq mathcal D represent the heartbeat, recovery, and activation dependencies.

10.2 Shared failure domains
The implementation SHALL record both direct shared dependencies and correlated failure domains.
For example, separate processes may still share one host, storage service, or scheduler.

10.3 Fault-domain mapping
FaultDomains:mathcal D -> power-set(mathcal F)
Dependencies may map to multiple fault domains.

10.4 Declaration completeness
Missing dependencies discovered through inspection or fault injection SHALL be treated as specification or conformance defects.

10.5 Isolation claims
Logical separation alone does not prove fault isolation.
Build 2's single-process implementation SHALL state its limitations explicitly.

11. Checkpointing, forced suspension, and restoration

11.1 Checkpoint identity
A checkpoint contains:
Checkpoint=(checkpointId,sourceInstance,sourceSession,sourceEpoch,manifestVersion,policyVersion,partitions,integrity,lineage)
Checkpoint identity is distinct from active execution-session identity.

11.2 Checkpoint classifications
Classification | Meaning
CONSISTENT | All required captured partitions satisfy the declared consistency contract
PARTIAL | One or more partitions are missing or intentionally excluded
TAINTED | Captured partitions contain unresolved consistency or integrity uncertainty
INVALID | Checkpoint fails minimum acceptance requirements
These classifications are not interchangeable.
A checkpoint may carry detailed per-partition classifications.

11.3 Ordinary suspension
Ordinary suspension SHALL:
1. Authorize the transition.
2. Quiesce required components.
3. Resolve or record in-flight operations.
4. Prepare checkpoint state.
5. Validate integrity.
6. Publish the checkpoint atomically.
7. Commit "SUSPENDED/NONE".

11.4 Forced suspension
If quiescence is impossible, an authorized forced-suspension procedure MAY:
1. Fence new protected writes.
2. Preserve trusted control-plane state.
3. Record unresolved external operations.
4. Mark unavailable or unverified cognitive partitions "PARTIAL" or "TAINTED".
5. Publish a validly classified preservation record.
6. Commit "SUSPENDED/NONE" when its required conditions hold.
Forced suspension SHALL NOT label unverified cognitive state as consistent.
If the minimum trusted control-plane preservation contract cannot be satisfied, the operation SHALL report failure and follow a separately defined stop or fault policy rather than falsely claiming successful suspension.

11.5 Restoration
Restoration SHALL validate:
- Administrative authority.
- Checkpoint identity and integrity.
- Partition classifications.
- Manifest and schema compatibility.
- Policy compatibility.
- Source-session and lineage references.
- Required recovery actions.
A "PARTIAL" or "TAINTED" checkpoint requires an explicit restoration policy.

11.6 Session allocation
An independently executing restored branch SHALL receive a fresh session identifier and valid fencing generation before activation.
An original-instance restart SHALL fence stale execution attempts.

11.7 No philosophical inference
Successful checkpoint restoration SHALL NOT automatically establish continuity of individual identity.

12. Event and transaction semantics

12.1 Governed event
A governed event contains:
Event=(id,type,target,payload,authContext,expectedVersion,sessionToken)
The trusted event envelope is distinct from untrusted payload content.

12.2 Idempotency
Identical authenticated replays MAY return the prior outcome without reexecution.
Conflicting event-ID reuse SHALL be rejected.

12.3 Accepted transition
An accepted transition SHALL:
1. Validate the event envelope.
2. Validate originator and executor authority.
3. Validate current state and expected version.
4. Validate current session fencing.
5. Validate time-sensitive guards.
6. Apply one atomic domain-state transition.
7. Record the committed outcome.

12.4 Rejected command
A rejected command SHALL NOT change protected domain state.
A separate append-only audit stream MAY record the rejected attempt.

12.5 Accepted failure transition
A trusted timeout, attempt failure, exhaustion, or component-fault event is an ordinary accepted state transition when its guards hold.
It is not modeled as an unauthorized rejected command.

12.6 External side effects
External effects SHALL be classified as completed, pending, cancelled, compensated, or unresolved under the applicable transaction contract.
Unresolved effects SHALL not silently disappear during suspension or restoration.

13. Safety invariants

13.1 Preserved Phase 0 invariants
The existing S0-01 through S0-30 register remains applicable, including:
- Exclusive constituent membership.
- Canonical ownership of protected state.
- Authorized custody.
- Prior-policy authorization.
- No payload-based privilege escalation.
- Idempotent replay handling.
- Immutable protected-state exposure.
- Valid lifecycle transitions.
- Transactional checkpoint publication.
- ADMIN CLASS protection.
- Bounded recovery and activation.
- Valid lifecycle–mode combinations.
- Session exclusivity.
- Authorized shutdown precedence.
Exact inherited wording SHALL be reconciled against the archived v1.1 and v1.2 registers before full-document freeze.

13.2 New or strengthened invariants
S0-31 — Clock-domain separation
Deadline predicates SHALL use trusted monotonic elapsed time, never the committed transition index.
S0-32 — Activation-producer independence
At least one authorized activation-request pathway SHALL exist that does not depend on recovery executing successfully.
S0-33 — Delegation-chain authorization
Protected ADMIN CLASS modifications SHALL require valid originator, executor, and delegation authority.
S0-34 — Canonical continuity session
Heartbeat runtime state SHALL reference the canonical session record without maintaining conflicting authoritative session identifiers or epochs.
S0-35 — Fenced protected commits
A protected commit SHALL NOT succeed under a stale execution-session token.
S0-36 — Atomic recovery accounting
Recovery attempt admission SHALL atomically consume or reserve the required attempt and resource budgets.
S0-37 — Exhaustion closure
An exhausted recovery cycle SHALL NOT resume automatically without separately authorized reset.
S0-38 — Deterministic functional mode
Every valid lifecycle and health-evidence combination SHALL derive exactly one permitted functional mode.
S0-39 — Checkpoint classification integrity
A checkpoint SHALL NOT be classified as consistent when required captured partitions fail their consistency contract.
S0-40 — Rejection-state isolation
Rejected commands SHALL NOT mutate protected domain state; accepted failure events remain explicit transitions.
S0-41 — Session restoration uniqueness
Independent restored branches SHALL NOT share one exclusive active execution-session identifier.
S0-42 — Bounded activation retry reachability
Permitted additional wake-up attempts SHALL remain reachable after earlier unacknowledged attempts, and no attempt SHALL exceed the configured limit.

14. Liveness and recoverability

14.1 Safety
For each permitted committed transition:
Valid(Sigma_k) AND Authorized(e_k,Sigma_k) IMPLIES Valid(Sigma_{k+1}).
This is a proof obligation.

14.2 Liveness
Under declared fairness, scheduling, clock, and resource assumptions:
always(P IMPLIES eventually Q)
may express required eventual progress.
For example, a valid overdue activation request with remaining budget should eventually be processed if the trusted timer, activation channel, and scheduler remain available.
No unconditional liveness guarantee is claimed under arbitrary infrastructure failure.

14.3 Recoverability
Define Recoverable(Sigma,f,mathcal A)
to mean that a permitted recovery path exists from state Sigma, under fault class f and assumptions mathcal A, to a state satisfying a specified restoration contract.
Recoverability is not implied by heartbeat activity.

14.4 Observational uncertainty
Unknown health evidence SHALL not be silently converted into healthy status.
The reference model SHALL distinguish observed health from actual fault conditions where the latter are known only to the test harness.

15. Verification and Build 2 conformance

15.1 Test register
Existing tests T0-01 through T0-52 remain the working register.
Build 2 SHALL prioritize the following additional adversarial tests.
ID | Test
T0-53 | Recovery fails before request creation; independent authorized producer submits request

2. NON-ROLLBACK AUTHORITY AND SESSION FENCING
2.1 Fencing Generation
For each instance (n), define:
[ E(n)\in\mathbb N ]
as the authoritative generation high-water mark.
Every issuance of new execution authority SHALL satisfy:
[ E_{\mathrm{new}}(n)>E_{\mathrm{previous}}(n) ]
Ordinary restoration SHALL NOT decrease (E(n)).
2.2 Current Authority
Define:
[ S(n)=(sessionId,generation,holder,status) ]
Where status is one of:
[ {ACTIVE,FENCED,REVOKED} ]
Only an ACTIVE authority record permits ordinary protected writes.
2.3 Protected Write Authorization
For a protected write event (e):
[ \begin{aligned} WriteAllowed(e,n)\iff{}& Authorized(e)\ &\land Lifecycle(n)=ACTIVE\ &\land e.sessionId=S(n).sessionId\ &\land e.generation=S(n).generation\ &\land S(n).status=ACTIVE\ &\land ResourceFenceAccepts(e) \end{aligned} ]
Administrative maintenance and shutdown operations use separately authorized control-plane transitions rather than ordinary cognitive write authority.
2.4 Resource-Side Fencing
A protected resource SHALL reject writes carrying a generation older than its trusted accepted-authority generation.
For the Build 2 reference model, resource-side fencing is simulated by an independently modeled protected-store guard.
This does not establish protection against rollback of the trusted store itself.
2.5 Checkpoint Restoration
A checkpoint's sourceSession and sourceEpoch are historical metadata.
They SHALL NOT directly become current authority.
Restoration performs:
1. Validate checkpoint integrity.
2. Validate current governance policy.
3. Validate current revocations.
4. Validate available budgets.
5. Fence prior execution authority.
6. Allocate fresh execution authority.
7. Restore permitted domain-state partitions.
8. Reconcile pending transactions and external effects.
9. Commit the new lifecycle state.
If the transaction fails before authority issuance, it must leave current authority unchanged or safely fenced.
If it fails after authority issuance, the issued generation remains consumed and cannot be reused.
2.6 Independent Branching
A restored independent branch SHALL receive:
• A fresh NNNC instance identifier.
• Fresh constituent component identifiers.
• A fresh execution-session identifier.
• A separately allocated fencing generation.
• A lineage reference to its source.
A branch SHALL NOT reuse the original instance's active membership identifiers.
2.7 Anti-Resurrection Invariant
For any event (e) created under authority (s_{\mathrm{old}}):
[ Fence(s_{\mathrm{old}}) \Rightarrow \Box\neg WriteAllowed(e) ]
This property holds under the assumption that trusted authority and resource-side fencing records are not rolled back.
It is a required safety property, not an established proof.

3. CLOCK-DOMAIN SEMANTICS
3.1 Trusted Clock Representation
Build 2 uses integer monotonic ticks:
[ Clock=(domainId,ticks) ]
Where:
[ ticks\in\mathbb N ]
A deadline is represented as:
[ Deadline=(domainId,dueTick) ]
3.2 Comparability
Two clock values may be directly compared only when their domain identifiers match.
A deadline from a previous clock domain SHALL NOT be evaluated against the current domain without an authorized rebase.
3.3 Restart Behavior
On a clock-domain restart:
1. The trusted control plane establishes a new clock-domain identifier.
2. Old timer deliveries are invalidated.
3. Open activation requests are cancelled or reconstructed under a new authorized request generation.
4. New deadlines are derived from declared remaining-duration or restart policy.
5. Reconstructed requests retain their incident-level consumed-budget history.
A restart SHALL NOT replenish wake-up allowances.
3.4 Suspension Behavior
During SUSPENDED, no ordinary heartbeat wake-up or recovery-attempt admission is permitted.
Time may continue advancing in the underlying substrate, but suspended-instance activation deadlines are invalidated or explicitly paused according to the suspension transaction.
On resumption, new deadlines must be established in the active clock domain.
3.5 Timer Delivery
A trusted timer event contains:
[ TimerEvent=(id,domainId,dueTick,observedTick) ]
A stale-domain timer event is rejected.
Timer expiry is evaluated at the committed transition point.
3.6 Timer Failure
The timer service is an explicit dependency and fault domain.
A lost timer event does not create an imaginary transition.
The implementation SHOULD support bounded periodic re-evaluation while the timer service is operational.
No deadline-response guarantee is claimed if all relevant timer-delivery mechanisms fail.

5.5 Deadline and Acknowledgement Ordering
For an acknowledgement:
[ AckValid(a,\tau) ]
requires a matching request generation, authenticated endpoint, and an open request.
An acknowledgement arriving after a nominal deadline may still be accepted if no competing terminal transition has committed and policy permits late acknowledgement.
The reference kernel SHALL use commit order to resolve the race.

No event may be authorized solely on a stale pre-commit snapshot.
5.6 Heartbeat Wake-Up
A heartbeat wake-up SHALL:
1. Validate the activation request.
2. Validate linked recovery eligibility.
3. Validate current lifecycle and session authority.
4. Reserve the wake-up budget.
5. Record the wake-up attempt.
6. Increment the wake-up count.
7. Establish the next deadline.
8. Emit the bounded activation command.
The activation remains AWAITING_ACK.
5.7 Wake-Up Exhaustion

At the final deadline, an unacknowledged request transitions to EXHAUSTED.
5.8 Acknowledgement Is Not Success
The following are distinct:
1. Activation acknowledged.
2. Recovery attempt admitted.
3. Restoration validated.
The heartbeat's activation supervision ends after a valid acknowledgement.
A subsequent recovery crash requires independently governed recovery handling.

6. CROSS-MACHINE SYNCHRONIZATION
6.1 Recovery Eligibility
Define:
[ RecoveryActivationEligible(q) ]
To require:
• The cycle exists.
• It is not terminal.
• The instance is ACTIVE.
• No shutdown or suspension fence is pending.
• The incident has available activation authority.
• The current session authority is valid.
• No conflicting activation request is open.
For a wake-up on an existing request, the last condition is evaluated as uniqueness of that same request, not absence of all requests.
6.2 Recovery-to-Activation Terminal Cascade
When a recovery cycle commits:
• SUCCEEDED: Linked open activation becomes COMPLETED.
• EXHAUSTED: Linked open activation becomes FAILED, unless already terminal.
• CANCELLED: Linked open activation becomes CANCELLED.
These changes are atomic.
A recovery cycle reaching FAILED is not automatically terminal if retry remains available.
6.3 Activation-to-Recovery Outcome
Activation exhaustion does not automatically exhaust the recovery cycle.
The recovery manager shall evaluate:
• Whether an attempt is already ACTIVE.
• Whether a trusted recovery endpoint accepted activation.
• Whether additional activation is permitted.
• Whether incident policy requires closure.
An ACTIVE recovery attempt may continue after activation exhaustion if it retains valid execution authority.
No new activation budget is created automatically.
6.4 Shutdown-Intent Fence
The control plane SHALL support a governed shutdown or suspension intent.
Once that intent commits:
• New heartbeat wake-ups are prohibited.
• New recovery attempts are prohibited.
• New ordinary protected cognitive writes are prohibited.
• Delayed acknowledgements cannot reactivate the instance.
Already admitted operations must be settled, cancelled, or recorded unresolved.
6.5 Final Lifecycle Transition
Before committing SUSPENDED, STOPPED, or TERMINATED, the control plane SHALL:
1. Fence ordinary execution authority.
2. Halt or mark unavailable the heartbeat.
3. Terminalize open activation requests.
4. Terminalize or account for open recovery cycles.
5. Classify unresolved transactions and effects.
6. Meet the required preservation or stop contract.
The final lifecycle transition is atomic with the required authoritative state updates.
6.6 Failed Shutdown
If the trusted control plane cannot establish the required fencing or preservation conditions, it SHALL NOT claim that a safe suspension or stop completed.
The failure must be recorded with a defined unresolved-control outcome.
6.7 Transition Ordering
Build 2 is a sequential reference model.
Events are processed in a total committed order.
Priority may guide scheduling, but correctness SHALL depend on commit-time guards and atomic transitions, not solely on a static priority list.

8. FUNCTIONAL-MODE DERIVATION
8.1 Effective Health
Health evidence is evaluated against the current trusted clock domain and freshness requirements.
Expired or untrusted evidence evaluates to UNKNOWN.
8.2 Heartbeat DEGRADED Semantics
For Build 2:
• HEALTHY: Full heartbeat contract satisfied.
• DEGRADED: Minimum heartbeat progress contract satisfied, but nonessential capability reduced.
• FAILED: Minimum heartbeat progress contract violated.
• UNKNOWN: Insufficient current trusted evidence.
8.3 Total Mode Function
For non-ACTIVE lifecycle:
[ Mode=NONE ]
For ACTIVE lifecycle, evaluate in order:
1. If heartbeat is FAILED or UNKNOWN, or cognition is UNKNOWN: FAULTED.
2. Else if cognition is FAILED: MINIMAL.
3. Else if cognition, heartbeat, or recovery is DEGRADED, or recovery is FAILED or UNKNOWN: DEGRADED.
4. Else: FULL.
This function is total for the 64 possible three-component health vectors.
8.4 Conformance Profiles
FULL: Heartbeat, recovery, and cognitive placeholder satisfy full required contracts.
DEGRADED: The minimum heartbeat contract and a usable cognitive capability remain, but one or more nonessential or recovery capabilities are degraded or unavailable.
MINIMAL: Cognition is unavailable, but the minimum heartbeat contract remains satisfied.
FAULTED: Required existential evidence or a required minimum contract is unavailable or violated.
FAULTED does not automatically mean that every component has stopped executing.
8.5 Mode Evaluation
The derived mode is evaluated:
• At relevant committed health transitions.
• At trusted evidence-expiry events.
• At lifecycle transitions.
• During conformance queries against current trusted time.
Mode is not independently writable.

[ INVALID>TAINTED>PARTIAL>CONSISTENT ]

9.2 Forced Suspension
Forced suspension may preserve healthy control-plane partitions while marking cognitive partitions TAINTED or PARTIAL.

9.3 Restoration Authority
Restoration cannot restore:
• Old fencing authority.
• Revoked permissions.
• Consumed budgets.
• Retired recovery cycles as active cycles.
• Prior event identifiers as unused.
• Stale clock-domain authority.
9.4 External Effects
Every unresolved external effect must carry a stable effect identifier.
On restoration, the system must distinguish:
• Completed.
• Pending.
• Cancelled.
• Compensated.
• Unknown.
An unknown effect SHALL NOT be blindly replayed as though it had never occurred.

10A.4 Trigger, Completion, Cancellation and Inclusive Deadlines
All state predicates are evaluated at the end of the given prefix. Cumulative occurrence predicates explicitly use exists over preceding steps. A q is bound once to its actual trigger occurrence and inherited prestate; forged trigger records do not activate it.
Triggered(q,h) iff exists o: Occurred(o,h) AND o.id=q.triggerID AND o.kind=TRIGGER AND o.instance=q.instance AND the independently interpreted trigger condition of q holds at that occurrence.
CompletionAt(q,h) is Some(i) for the earliest admitted actual occurrence at step i satisfying q's interpreted completion property after its trigger; otherwise None. CancellationAt and SupersessionAt similarly select actual authorized terminal operations under q's original version. Ties are resolved by inherited decision/commit order. No record alone supplies these witnesses.
Completed(q,h) iff CompletionAt(q,h) is Some(i). Cancelled and Superseded analogously mean that their admitted terminal witness has occurred by the endpoint. Late completion may be recorded but does not make a deadline met. Terminal winners and the acceptance of late acknowledgements follow the inherited request/lifecycle rules; an expired deadline is not an invented cancellation.
Applicable(q,h) iff Triggered(q,h) AND no authorized cancellation/supersession has become the applicable terminal winner. Completion does not erase applicability for historical deadline assessment. Support unavailability does NOT make this end-to-end obligation disappear. MechanismEnabled excludes neither q's own failure to run nor its own failure to complete; those failures cannot falsify the antecedent by definition.
due(q):=(q.domain,q.start+q.delta), a Clock. Comparison of clocks uses Section 3.2: tick comparisons are defined only within the same valid domain. TimingAssumptions(q,h) means the stipulated clock/response-unit assumptions hold at the endpoint; it does not include completion.
DeadlineClosed(q,h) iff an actual admitted DEADLINE_CLOSE decision for q has occurred with matching-domain tick >= due(q).tick, OR an actual matching-domain clock observation at a tick strictly greater than due(q).tick has occurred. At the exact due tick, an unclosed prefix stays pending, allowing same-tick completion before the expiry decision under inherited ordering. Completion at the due tick is within bound if admitted before the terminal expiry decision. A late acknowledgement may be legally accepted by policy while still being late for this timing property.
TerminalBeforeDeadline(q,h) means an authorized cancellation/supersession won before the first deadline-close point and before an admitted timely completion; it does not erase any earlier miss. This defined abbreviation has type Obligation x History -> Bool.
TimelyCompletion(q,h) means CompletionAt(q,h)=Some(i), the completion is admitted before a conflicting terminal winner, clock_i.domain=q.domain and clock_i.tick<=due(q).tick. This defined abbreviation has type Obligation x History -> Bool.
EndToEndMet(q,h) iff Triggered(q,h) AND TimelyCompletion(q,h).
EndToEndMissed(q,h) iff Triggered(q,h) AND DeadlineClosed(q,h) AND NOT EndToEndMet(q,h) AND NOT TerminalBeforeDeadline(q,h).
EndToEndPending(q,h) iff Triggered(q,h) AND NOT EndToEndMet(q,h) AND NOT DeadlineClosed(q,h) AND NOT TerminalBeforeDeadline(q,h).
EndToEndOutcome is UNTRIGGERED if not Triggered; else MET if EndToEndMet; else MISSED if EndToEndMissed; else CANCELLED/SUPERSEDED according to TerminalBeforeDeadline's winner; else PENDING. A cancelled obligation is not a missed one merely because time later advances. A cancellation after a closed missed deadline cannot remove the miss. A clock-domain change without a valid inherited reconciliation cannot establish a deadline close in the old domain.

SupportCondition(q,h) iff every relevant promise condition for Dependencies(q), including the required scheduling/timer/resource conditions, actually holds at the endpoint according to its interpreted service semantics. A delivery promise, request or acceptance record is not a delivered service fact.
GoodAt(q,h):=Applicable(q,h) AND NOT Cancelled(q,h) AND NOT Superseded(q,h) AND SupportCondition(q,h) AND MechanismEnabled(q,h) AND TimingAssumptions(q,h).
For h0 Prefix h, GoodPrefix(q,h0,h):= forall g with h0 Prefix g Prefix h: GoodAt(q,g). Its type is declared in 10A.3. It does not refer to Completed, any outcome assessment or the desired conformance conclusion.
For every finite interpreted execution rho, every q triggered at h_a, and every j with a<=j<len(rho), the required bounded-response property is:
  GoodPrefix(q,h_a,h_j) AND DeadlineClosed(q,h_j) IMPLIES EndToEndMet(q,h_j).
This is a property to verify for a conforming mechanism under delivered support, not an axiom imposed on all traces. Adequate support plus no completion supplies a genuine violating countermodel. Scheduler starvation falsifies a support antecedent, so it does NOT falsify this implication; it can still yield EndToEndMissed independently. This corrects the review's suggested starvation counterexample.
Bounded service delivery and clock progression must be independently interpreted. Fairness without a numerical bound cannot imply finite deadline completion. Opportunity-count bounds require a separate counter and explicit opportunity semantics; they are not silently converted into clock ticks. This issue's executable reference subset uses one declared clock-domain interval per obligation. 10A.6 Promise, Observation, Independent Assessment and Attribution
Assess(z,F)=ESTABLISHED iff AdequatePositive(z,F) AND NOT AdequateNegative(z,F); REFUTED iff AdequateNegative(z,F) AND NOT AdequatePositive(z,F); UNKNOWN otherwise. Thus inconsistent evidence is not both true and false, and empty evidence establishes nothing.
ObservedSupport(q,h,F) applies this polarity rule to valid observation records about SupportClaim(q,h), without claiming independence. SupportAssessment(q,h,F)=Assess(SupportClaim(q,h),F) only when ObservationCovers and IndependentEnough hold for that claim; otherwise UNKNOWN. These are evidence-relative judgments under the declared trust model, not unconditional entailments of semantic truth. PromisedSupport is declarative; SupportCondition is actual fulfillment; ObservedSupport is observed evidence; SupportAssessment is independently qualified assessment. None silently replaces another.
ObligationAssessment(q,h,F) returns a semantic outcome value only when the corresponding OutcomeClaim(q,h) is independently supported with adequate temporal coverage and no material contrary evidence; otherwise UNKNOWN. It must not infer MISSED merely from an absent completion report. Its interpretation must be sound relative to the stipulated evidence assumptions before a model result is transferred to reality.
SupportBreachClaim concerns an applicable provider promise actually unsatisfied in the relevant interval, not merely a missing self-report. MechanismBreachClaim concerns an unsatisfied mechanism guarantee under established enabling support or another independently specified mechanism-fault witness. Support failure can be outside a provider's stated scope without proving a provider breach.
Attributions(q,h,F) contains SUPPORT_BREACH iff its breach claim is independently ESTABLISHED; contains MECHANISM_BREACH iff its claim is independently ESTABLISHED; contains UNRESOLVED if the assessed outcome is MISSED/UNKNOWN and material attribution remains open. Both established breach types may coexist; unresolved attribution can coexist with a known partial cause. OutcomeAssessment and attribution are different outputs. A support miss is not automatically a mechanism breach or a provider-contract breach.

The prestate and operation payload are those of the inherited transition, not caller-supplied substitutes. A support finding adds no disjunct permitting admission. No support contract field can alter Pi's permissions, revocations, generation, accounting or replay rules. Mode remains the Section 8 derived function of lifecycle and effective health; support evidence may inform health only under the governing evidence contract.
UpdateAllowed(c,c',h) iff c.instance=c'.instance AND c'.version>c.version AND an actual prior-policy-authorized CONTRACT_CHANGE operation binds c' at h. ContractOf(q), its original bound/domain/completion semantics and past outcomes remain immutable for existing q. Authorized replacements use a fresh q' linked by a recorded supersession or reconciliation; they do not overwrite q.
For prefixes h Prefix g: prior audit records remain a prefix of later audit records, q's original contract binding stays equal, and any established historical deadline miss remains a miss for that obligation. New evidence may append a reassessment of whether a prior report was mistaken, retaining the original report, governing version, evidence and supersession rationale; new policy alone cannot turn the old actual miss into success. Shutdown intent fences ordinary activation under S0-46 even when progress is pending. Unavailable enforcement does not establish successful physical containment.
